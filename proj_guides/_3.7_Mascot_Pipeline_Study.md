# The mascot: from a picture to Azu in the app -- study (2026-11-03)

How a picture of a mascot becomes the guide's character in the app:
- as a button;
- talking, with moods;
- pointing;
- its body behind a UI Toolkit block, its head over the block's edge and an arm in front pointing into the block;
- walking on the wall in AR.

The developer's pictures are in `proj_guides/mascote/`: a white-and-cobalt azulejo with big eyes, a hat with a blue feather,
noodle arms and feet and a Belém tower; a tabby cat with a red neckerchief, a backpack, a tram and a pastel de nata.

Related: `_3.4_The_Guide_Study.md` section 4.11 (the tile of light, the hand-off to the wall, MCPs); the Lab's `v8q` (the
shader tile).

Research: one subagent (2026-11-03) + the Architect. "(unverified)" = a third-party or conflicting source.

## 1. One mascot contract, several sources
- **The contract:** `SetMood`, `SetViseme` (Adobe Character Animator's 11 visemes as the names: Aa, D, Ee, F, L, M, Oh, R, S,
  Uh, W-Oo), `PointAt`, `Walk / Hop`, `Layer(Back / Front)`.
- **The sources:** each is an adapter behind the contract, so the options add up instead of competing.
- **The Editor:**
  - "Mascot source: Built-in (Tile of light) / Rive file / 3D model (GLB/FBX) / Sprite sheet".
  - **Import…** checks the clips (idle, talk, point, walk, the moods), the viseme names, a front-arm part and the scale. Its
    messages name the parts.
  - **Generate from a picture with:** Meshy / Tripo / Rodin / Animated Drawings (frames) / Build in Blender (the agent). Each
    produces one of the sources.

## 2. Picture -> 2D character
- **Rive:** a raster picture meshed and bound to bones; state machines plus data binding (a mood enum, a viseme number) suit
  visemes and moods.
  - In Unity, a Rive Panel renders to a render texture (RT); Canvas Renderer shows it in uGUI and Texture Renderer puts it on
    any mesh (an AR quad). UI Toolkit is not supported: an RT bridge.
  - The official Rive MCP (in the desktop Editor) builds artboards, bones, state machines, data binding and Luau scripts, but
    not meshes, and nothing turns a picture into a rig.
  - Free exports with a splash; Cadet is $9/seat/month.
- **Spine:** no official AI rigging (community tools, unverified).
  - spine-unity offers `SkeletonAnimation` (a world mesh, AR), `SkeletonGraphic` (uGUI) and an experimental UI Toolkit package.
  - `SkeletonRenderSeparator` splits the draw order, so the arm can go in front.
- **Live2D Cubism 5.x:** AI face and body deformers from a layered PSD (no one-click picture-to-model). A Unity SDK with
  MotionSync lip-sync; students get 76 % off (check).
- **Moho Pro 14.4 (a key find):** exports glTF/GLB with bones, actions as clips and Smart Bones as morph targets. A real-time 2D
  rig that reaches Unity by GLB (glTFast supports skins and morphs).
- **Picture-to-frames tools (sprite-sheet factories only):**
  - Meta Animated Drawings: MIT, archived; frames and joints, no visemes.
  - Reallusion Cartoon Animator 5 and Adobe Character Animator: export video or PNG only.
  - AI video (Hedra, Kling, Runway, Viggle): pre-rendered, no rig, cannot say arbitrary lines.

## 3. Picture -> 3D character
- **Meshy:** FBX/GLB/USDZ.
  - Free: 100 credits/month, outputs CC BY 4.0. Pro €19 (half price the first month).
  - The rigging API is humanoid only.
  - Its education programme is reported closed to new users (conflicting).
- **Tripo:** Rig v2.5 handles non-humanoids (quadruped and more), GLB/FBX. The best AI option for the cat.
- **Rodin Gen-2:** quad-dominant meshes; an education plan of about $12/month (unverified).
- **Hunyuan3D 3.x:** its licence may exclude the EU (verify before use).
- **Kaedim:** too expensive.
- **The tile character is the generators' worst case** (a lumpy slab, the face baked in, the limbs fused). It is cleaner and
  faster for an agent to build it in Blender:
  - a bevelled box with the concept art projected on the front;
  - bendy-bone arms and feet;
  - a modelled hat and feather;
  - a face atlas (eyes and mouth visemes) on the face, or a Rive face drawn by Texture Renderer.
- **The cat:** Meshy or Tripo, then retopology, the Tripo quadruped rig and shape keys.
- **Blender cleanup (blender-mcp):**
  1. Retopology (QuadriFlow, voxel remesh, Instant Meshes).
  2. UVs and a bake.
  3. A rig (Rigify, Auto-Rig Pro quadruped).
  4. Shape keys and actions (idle, talk, moods, point, walk / hop).
  5. **The front arm as its own object on the same armature.**
  6. Export FBX or GLB.

## 4. Body behind the block, head above its edge, arm in front
- **Recommended: one rig and one animation clock, drawn in two layers.**
  - Body meshes on layer `MascotBack`, the front arm on `MascotFront`.
  - Two cameras with the same transform and projection, each with its own culling mask, render two small RTs (256-512 px).
  - In UI Toolkit, `mascotBack` sits before the card in the hierarchy and `mascotFront` after it, both with the same rect in one
    container anchored to the card's top edge; the front one has `pickingMode = Ignore`.
  - One Animator poses the skeleton once a frame and both cameras draw that pose, so the layers never drift apart. At rest,
    render on demand (`Camera.Render()`).
  - For a translucent card, `mascotBack` sits in an `overflow:hidden` box whose bottom is the card's top edge (as the Lab's
    `v8q` peek does).
- **Other ways:**
  - a single RT with a card-shaped mask (fragile: it also cuts the arm);
  - UI Shader Graph (URP only; a darkening bug, IN-134952), better kept for the glaze and hologram looks;
  - Rive: two artboards (Body, FrontArm) on one view model, ticked together (unverified);
  - sprite sheets: two or three VisualElements (body, front arm, mouth) stepping their frames from one `schedule.Execute`
    ticker, native and with no RT.

## 5. Walking in AR
- **On the wall's surface:** parallel to the tracked wall, about 5 mm off it, hopping from tile to tile. It needs only the wall
  plane already tracked.
  - Walking on the floor in front of the wall needs horizontal plane detection, which often fails up close. It is an option (a
    good fit for the cat), with a fallback "ledge" along the wall rectangle's bottom.
- **The hand-off from the button:**
  1. A ray through the button's point; spawn at d ≈ 0.35 m.
  2. Height h = pixel height · d / f_y, with f_y = Screen.height / (2 · tan(fov/2)).
  3. The same animation frame, and the UI mascot hidden in the same frame.
  4. A Bézier arc to the wall over about 1 s, turning to the wall and scaling to real size (a tile is about 14-15 cm).
- **Scale:** real scale, but at least 48 px on the screen: s = max(1, minPx / projectedPx).
- **Shadow:** a blob quad on the wall plane, offset along a fixed top-left light. Not URP decals (mobile problems).
- **Occlusion:** none in front of the wall; a stencil quad to walk "into" it.
- **The way back:** to the nearest wall point, the camera point under the button, shrink and swap. If tracking is lost, a
  "poof" back to the button.
- **Every source works on a world quad or mesh:** sprites, Spine `SkeletonAnimation`, Live2D, a Rive Texture Renderer, 3D.

## 7. Recommended pipeline
1. **A 3D model (GLB/FBX), built by the agent.**
   - The tile: procedurally in Blender with blender-mcp from the picture (a face atlas, a split front arm, FBX).
   - The cat: Meshy or Tripo, then cleanup.
   - One asset serves the UI (two culling-mask RTs) and AR (real walking and a shadow).
2. **A Rive file:** the best 2D look and talk. The agent cuts the picture into parts and the Rive MCP builds bones, state
   machines and data binding; the developer polishes the weights and timing.
3. **A sprite sheet:** the universal fallback and the import target for AI video and Animated Drawings (body, front-arm and
   mouth atlases).

- **The developer, by hand:** the concept and turnaround views, the accounts, the look and timing by eye, AR on a device, the
  Rive weights.
- **The agent:** Blender modelling, rig, shape keys, actions and export (blender-mcp); Meshy (its MCP) and the Tripo API; the
  Rive rig (Rive MCP); Unity import, layers, the two cameras, prefabs, Animator and tests (Unity MCP).
- **Accounts:**
  - Blender: free.
  - Meshy: Free (CC BY) or Pro €19.
  - Tripo: free tier (check its commercial terms).
  - Rive: Free / Cadet $9.
  - Optional: Rodin education, Live2D student.
  - Avoid Hunyuan (licence territory) and Kaedim (cost).

  squared); a generator wants one object.

## 10. Built: Azu, the tile, in 3D (2026-11-03)

- **Files:** `proj_guides/mascote/3d/azu_tile.blend` (the source) and `azu_tile.glb` (0.7 MB); `tile_face.png` is the
  picture's tile with the eyes and the mouth painted out (they are their own meshes).
- **Model:** a bevelled slab (1 x 0.24 x 1; 1 unit = one tile, about 0.15 m on a wall) with a cobalt edge, the face picture on
  the front, eyes (navy iris, pale rim, two highlights), a mouth, a fedora with a navy band and a blue feather, noodle arms
  with mittens, short legs with feet, and an inverted-hull cobalt outline.
- **Rig:** root, body, hat, upperarm / forearm / hand on each side, leg on each side. The front arm (`Azu_ArmFront`, the
  character's left) is its own mesh on the same skeleton (section 4).
- **Clips (24 fps):** idle (48), talk (24), listen (48), think (48), point (24, held), walk (24, a hop per step), wave (24).
- **Morphs (set at run time, not in the clips):** Blink on the eyes; Open, Wide and Round on the mouth (the visemes: A = Open,
  E = Wide + a little Open, O / W-Oo = Round, M = none).
- **In the Lab:** Mascot source = 3D model (GLB) (`v8w`, `_3.3` section 11).
- **Next:** the cat (a generator, then the same rig steps); Unity import (glTFast) and the two-layer render; the tile of
  light's styles in the Editor.

## 11. Built: the developer's Meshy models as mascots (2026-11-03)

- **Sources:** the developer made Azu, the mouse and the explorer on Meshy's web (Meshy 7.1, CC BY 4.0) from
  `proj_guides/mascote/*.png`; the raw files are in `3d/meshy/` (Azu and the mouse textured, about 90,000 and 190,000
  triangles; the explorer shape only: no UVs, no colour).
- **One script builds each:** `3d/rig_meshy.py` (in Blender: `exec(open(...).read()); build("mouse")`, one model per fresh file,
  since the clips' names are the animations' names):
  1. import, then reduce to 22,000-24,000 triangles. Azu keeps its texture seams apart (welding them let the reduction fold
     triangles across them and smeared the face); the mouse and the explorer are welded first.
  2. the explorer: UVs, then a 1024 px texture painted from `explorer.png` by front projection; the back of the head gets the
     curls' cream, the back of the coat the coat beside the waistcoat, the hat's top and back plain navy.
  3. a skeleton placed from landmarks on an 800 px front render (root, body, head, arm.L/R, leg.L/R; the mouse's tail in
     three). Weights: the tile by rigid parts at the slab's measured edges (a ceramic slab must not bend); the mouse and the
     explorer by Blender's heat weights, each arm cut back to what lies near its bone (the coat followed the arm as a sheet).
  4. the front arm (the character's left, x+) cut out as `*_ArmFront`; the cut runs where both sides carry the same weights,
     so the seam never opens.
  5. the face: lids in the face's own colour (sampled from the texture above the eye) with a thin lash line, the shape key
     Blink; a dark mouth with Open, Wide and Round; both lie on the surface (ray cast) and have no area at rest.
  6. the seven clips, keyed per bone in armature space. An arm modelled raised (Meshy's Azu waves) hangs like the other arm
     at rest. The tile's body takes the head's turns (its face is on its body). Arms fused into a coat keep within about 65°
     of hanging: point at about 50°, wave out to the side.
  7. the size baked into the bones and meshes (every node at scale 1; three.js frames a skinned mesh by its geometry's box,
     which a scaled parent node puts off): Azu's slab is one tile, the others as tall as Azu (1.5). Textures at 1024 px
     (colour, normal) and 512 px (roughness), JPEG.
- **Checked by eye:** a contact sheet of each clip at two moments, the face's morphs, and the Lab's own canvas (`test8x.js`).
  The mouse's sleeve still stretches a little at the end of a wave; a sleeve split from the coat would fix it (by hand).
- **In the Lab:** Mascot source = 3D model (GLB) > Model (`v8x`, `_3.3` section 11): Azu detailed (default), Azu simple,
  the mouse, the explorer.
- **Next:** the explorer's own Meshy texture, if the developer re-downloads it with colour (then `paint` is skipped); the
  poet and the soldier (`poet.png`, `soldier.png`) by the same route; Unity import (glTFast).
