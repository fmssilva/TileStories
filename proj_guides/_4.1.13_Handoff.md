## 1 · What the project is
- **TileStories:** a Unity 6.3 AR app (URP, UI Toolkit) over the "Grande Panorama de Lisboa", the azulejo panel at the Museu Nacional do Azulejo. Visitors point their phone at the wall: points of interest (POIs) glow, and a tap opens their story, 3D, 360°, Then & now, tours and games.
- **The World Editor Lab:** one self-contained HTML page that prototypes the app and its editor.
  - **Left:** a phone or tablet showing the app over the wall, in portrait or landscape.
  - **Right:** a Unity-Inspector-style editor with the tabs App, Global Scene, Specific Marker, Experiences and Decisions.
  - Every config can be tried live. The Lab is the spec for the Unity build.

## 4 · The working rules (the user's standing rules)
- **"Be good, not fast."** Quality over speed, always. Plan in detail; confirm every section and subsection.
- **Everything must be implementable in Unity.** No prototype technique Unity can't do. `tests/unity_lint.py` enforces it: strict on `kids_*.css` and `prem_*.css`; on the shared `ds.css` / `tap.css` (not yet mapped, `_4.1.9d` §6) a ratchet, `tests/unity_lint_ratchet.json`, whose counts may only go down.
  - **Allowed:** radius, borders, 9-slice border-image (Unity `-unity-slice-*`), sprites, 2D translate/rotate/scale, named easings, one text-shadow.
  - **Tagged only:** soft shadows (`/* unity: sprite */`), gradients (`/* unity: ramp */`), keyframes (`tween:`), the camera glass (`/* unity: camera-glass */`).
  - **Banned:** backdrop-filter (except the camera glass), filter, mask, mix-blend, clip-path, 3D transforms, cubic-bezier (except `--k-back`), `:has()`, `inherit`, dashed borders.
- **For every feature, in this order:**
  1. Implement in the parts.
  2. Build.
  3. Run deep visual checks with critic agents. Cover phone and tablet, portrait and landscape, light and dark, and every profile (Premium and Playful, text L).
  4. Run regress and the sweeps, then re-baseline.
  5. Republish over the same artifact.
  6. Update the session log and the guides (version 0).
  7. Save to the PC.
- **Editor conventions (Unity):**
  - foldouts, starting collapsed;
  - ReorderableList for named lists;
  - Prefab-style overrides (bold + blue bar + ↺);
  - sliders for ranges;
  - clicking a config shows its screen on the phone.
- **Accessibility and AR:**
  - text at least 13 pt, with body contrast at least 7:1;
  - touch targets at least 48 pt;
  - selection never shown by colour alone;
  - nothing clipped or overlapping, at text L too;
  - at least 75 % of the wall visible (55 % with a card open);
  - one motion at a time; celebrations ≤ 1.5 s.
- **Communication:** reply to the user in **European Portuguese**. Keep the user's email private.

## 5 · How the big rounds were run (a method that works)
- **Inventory and contact sheets:**
  - `tools/review/inventory.py` lists every editor fold.
  - `section_shots.py` makes one sheet per fold: 6 profile rows (premium light, premium dark, playful light, playful dark, premium text L, playful text L) × devices.
  - `state_shots.py` does the same for named app states.
- **Parallel review + fix agents**, each owning its own files so they never collide:
  - Flows → `kids_50` / `prem_flows`
  - Foundations → `kids_00`, `kids_10` / `prem_foundations`
  - Surfaces → `kids_20` / `prem_surfaces`
  - Main → `kids_30` / `prem_main`
  - Tap → `kids_40` / `prem_tap`
  - Wall → `33_markers.js`

  Changes outside an agent's files go in as proposals, which the main session merges. See `tools/review/AGENT_PROMPT.md` and `BRIEF_v20_example.md`.
- **After merging:** an independent critic who did not do the work, then one fixer for the blockers, then the full safety net, then publish.
