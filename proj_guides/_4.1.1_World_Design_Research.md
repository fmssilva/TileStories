## 1. Where we start (verified on disk)

- A POI is a **3D point** (`POIData.position`, Immersal map frame) plus a marker (shape, icon, ring, badge, effects, label,
  leader line under displacement). Nothing in the framework yet uses **the shape of the wall**.
- **The wall's shape already exists as an asset:** Immersal exports a textured mesh of each map in the SAME frame as the POI
  positions (`146267-LivingRoom2-tex.glb`, today filed under `Apps/LivingRoom/Resources/LivingRoom/CardMedia/models/` and only
  used as a card 3D demo). It is not loaded in the Scene view or under the mock camera. This mesh is the natural "art space"
  of this domain (section 4.2).
- The three real walls (`Apps/Panorama`, `Apps/Chafariz`, `Apps/Mural`) hold no picture and no map of the wall yet. The only
  wall that can be tested is LivingRoom, which has no painting on it (fixed by step 0).
- Already built and reusable: selection events (`SelectionEventBus`), reveal / pulse / halo effects, leader lines, LOD and
  clusters, live Play Mode config, the Editor rig in the Scene view, the mock camera, `reduce_motion`, glTFast (GLB), and an
  empty `UI/World/GuideCharacter/` folder (the mascot planned in `_0_work_plan.md` Stage 3).
- `_0_work_plan.md` already plans: a lapse / epoch timeline (Panorama: pre-1755 / earthquake / Pombaline / today; Mural:
  time of day), a short 2D earthquake sequence (Stage 7 stretch), an optional per-wall occlusion mesh (Stage 7), 3D landmark
  models (Stage 7). World Design must plug into those, not duplicate them.

## 2. Critical review of the earlier chat (`_4.1` notes)

What it got right:
- Store artwork as **data per POI**, not code per building, and keep a generic renderer. Matches the framework's rules.
- Start with **outlines made of points**, not a full Paint. Vectors (a list of points), editable by dragging a point. Correct.
- Lines saved in coordinates **relative to the wall, not the screen**. Correct, and it is the key idea.

What it got wrong or never said:
1. **It proposed building a 2D Paint over a flat image** with its own coordinate system. The wall already exists in 3D (the
   Immersal textured mesh) in the very frame the app localises in, and Unity's Scene view already is a "Paint/Blender" for
   positioned content: Splines (a pen tool whose points snap to a mesh surface), the Move tool, the Animation window,
   Timeline, the Particle System, Shader Graph. Rebuilding those inside the POI Editor would be months of work for a worse
   copy, and a framework the developer must learn (section 4).
2. **It never mentioned alignment error.** Immersal places content to a few centimetres, and the error changes as you walk
   along a 23-40 m wall (unverified for these walls: it must be measured). A marker 5 cm off is invisible. A 1 px gold
   hairline around a window 5 cm off looks broken. So effects must be graded by how much precision they need (section 4.6).
3. **It never mentioned the quality of the reference.** The Immersal mesh texture is photogrammetry: good enough to see where
   a building is, maybe too soft to trace a window frame. To be measured on the poster map (step 0); the fallback is a
   high-resolution photo laid onto the mesh in the Editor only (section 4.2).
4. **Nothing on lighting.** The camera image changes exposure all the time. Content that *pastes pixels* over the wall (a
   coloured photo of the castle) never matches the live image. Content that *modulates the live image* does: darken the
   rest, add light, tint, outline. This is the most important taste rule found (section 4.1).
5. **Nothing on rights, performance, testing on a real painting, or whether "wow" helps or hurts learning** (section 8).

## 3. The "wow" catalogue (condensed)

The premium look across projection mapping, museum AR (ReBlink at the AGO, Rijksmuseum "Operation Night Watch", Artivive)
and Apple's visionOS guidance comes from **restraint**: the painting stays the hero; motion is slow (300-600 ms, ease-out),
small (a few degrees, a few pixels) and staggered; one colour of light (warm white or gold); edges feathered; sound quiet.
Projection mapping on building facades is the closest visual language to this domain: it is exactly "light and motion placed
on the real architecture".

## 4. Recommended architecture: "World content is ordinary Unity content, placed on the wall mesh"

### 4.0 The principle (answers "paint tool or slots?" and "how does a normal Unity developer work?")
A Unity developer who makes positioned AR content today does this: puts a reference of the real place in the scene, creates
GameObjects on top of it with the Move tool, draws paths with **Splines**, animates with the **Animation window** or
**Timeline**, adds **Particle Systems**, writes looks in **Shader Graph**, saves the result as a **prefab**, and lets code
decide when it plays. That is already a complete "Paint / Blender" for positioned content, documented, taught everywhere, and
free.

So the framework does NOT build an editor for art. It adds the four things Unity does not know about this project:
1. **the wall as reference** in the Scene view (and in Play Mode under the mock camera);
2. **a home per POI** for its world content, kept in the right place relative to the wall and the POI;
3. **when things play**: the POI's own events (revealed, in view, selected, card opened, card closed, idle) start an Animator
   state, a Timeline or a particle system;
4. **a few ready-made "wow" components** (outline, region, spotlight, light sweep...) that work on an ordinary Unity spline,
   so 80 % of the effects need no animation at all, only a spline and three sliders.

A developer who knows Unity learns two or three components and one rule ("put it under the POI's world content"). A developer
who does not know Unity learns Unity, which is a skill worth having, not a private framework. This is the hybrid answer:
**everything positioned is authored with Unity's own tools on the wall mesh; the framework adds the wall, the triggers, the
presets and the guard-rails.** Art made elsewhere (Photoshop PSB layers, video, Blender GLB, Rive) enters as an ordinary
Unity asset inside that prefab, the same way a PNG or a sprite does.

### 4.1 Taste rule: modulate the camera image, don't paste over it
Default effects darken, lighten, tint or outline the live image through a region (multiply / screen / additive blends). They
inherit the real wall's exposure and colour, so they look as if they belong in the room, and they hide a few cm of error.
Pasted pixels (images, video, cut-outs) are the exception, used when the content IS new imagery (then-and-now, colourise,
animation), always tight to a region and with a soft edge.

### 4.2 The wall reference: the Immersal textured mesh
- Per wall, the textured GLB Immersal exports with the map (same frame as `POIData.position`), filed as a wall asset (not a
  card media file), shown under the rig in the Scene view by a POI Editor toggle, and under the mock camera in Play Mode so
  the Editor shows the real painting. **Never rendered on a device**: there the real camera image is the wall.
- On a device the same mesh can be useful **invisible**: a depth-only occluder so a 3D model can rise "out of" the wall (D2),
  and a surface to ray-cast taps against (A3).
- If its texture is too soft to trace windows (measured in step 0), an Editor-only high-resolution photo is laid onto the
  mesh for tracing. A refinement, not step 1.

### 4.3 Lines and regions = Unity Splines (the developer's "how is a line stored?")
A line is a `SplineContainer` (package `com.unity.splines`): an ordered list of knots, each a 3D point with optional tangents
(curves). It is drawn with the **Draw Splines Tool**: each click on the wall mesh drops a knot ON the surface (verified,
Splines manual); click-drag makes a curve; knots are moved, inserted and deleted with Unity's own handles and undo. It is
saved inside the POI's prefab, relative to the wall. A closed spline is a region. Nothing new to learn and nothing to build.

The framework's components read that spline: they sample it into a mesh -- a fill for a region, a ribbon for a line whose
UV.x is "distance along the line" -- so one shader parameter draws the line on, and the cross-section gives soft edges and
glow. One closed spline per POI is reused by the spotlight, the outline, the fill, the tap area and the clipping of any
imported art.

Optional helper, outside Unity: SAM 2 (Segment Anything, Apache-2.0) can turn "click on the building in a photo" into a
contour; a later script could turn that into knots. Never required.

### 4.4 Per POI: the World Content prefab
- A POI may have a **world content** prefab, edited in Unity's **Prefab Mode in Context** (Unity shows the wall and the other
  POIs around while you edit). The POI Editor row has a field, a "Create" and an "Edit" button that open it. The config
  stores only the prefab's key, like card media today.
- At runtime the framework instantiates it under the localised wall root, at the same place it was authored, hides it with
  LOD, and switches it to its static state under `reduce_motion`.
- Inside, anything Unity allows. A small **World Moment** component maps POI events to what plays (an Animator trigger, a
  `PlayableDirector`, a particle system, a framework preset), with a dropdown of the closed set of events. Sequencing is a
  Timeline, which is Unity's own sequencer; no private step list.
- **Presence**, per POI (default from its hierarchy level, like effects today): `marker | region | both` -- with `region` the
  painted building itself is what you tap, and the marker shows only from far away (LOD).

### 4.5 Wall-level content
Things that belong to the wall, not to a POI (birds over the city, water, the lock-on line, a time-of-day grade, a guide
character's paths): one **wall world content** prefab with the same rules, listening to wall events (localised, lapse /
epoch changed, idle).

### 4.6 Ready-made components ("presets"), graded by precision
The framework's own look, each a component on a spline + a Shader Graph material + 3-5 Inspector fields + a `reduce_motion`
static state: **Outline** (draw-on, width, colour, glow), **Region** (fill, tint, tap area), **Spotlight** (dims the rest of
the camera image outside the region), **Light Sweep**, **Breathing Glow**, **Tile-Grid Reveal**, **Shimmer**. Each declares
how much precision it needs (low / med / high); the POI Editor warns when a high-precision preset sits on a wall whose
measured error (step 0) is too large. Every field also appears in the POI Editor so the framework rule (every option has an
Editor row, an (i), a default, live Play Mode) still holds; the Inspector and the POI Editor edit the same component.

### 4.7 What stays external, by design
Brushes, layers, keyframes, bones and 3D modelling: Photoshop / Krita (PSB layers -> Unity 2D Animation), After Effects ->
video, Blender -> GLB, Rive -> Rive Unity runtime. Rule for the future: an in-framework feature is added only if it needs
the wall, the POI or its events. Every hosted AR editor that tried to be everything (Adobe Aero, Meta Spark, 8th Wall's
hosted editor) closed in 2025-2026; a framework made of plain Unity assets cannot be switched off.

### 4.8 Where world content data lives -- option C, "mixed by type" (confirmed by the developer, 2026-10-29)
| Content | Lives in | Authored with | Live in Play Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lines, regions, presets (outline, spotlight, sweep, tile reveal...): ~80 % | `config.json` (knots in the map frame + preset fields) | Unity's Draw Splines Tool on the wall mesh in the Scene view; "Save All" writes the knots, exactly as `CapturePositions` writes marker positions today | yes |
| Real animation (Timeline, Animator, particles, PSB rigs, video, animated GLB) | a prefab in the wall's content folder, referenced from `config.json` by path | Unity's own tools | re-spawned on save |
Options rejected: A (everything in prefabs: the wall stops being data, only sliders live, weaker thesis claim) and B
(everything in JSON: real animation would need private tools copying Timeline). The two "homes" meet through the region: a
prefab can be clipped by its POI's region, which the framework hands to it.

### 4.9 Where files live: app vs framework, and a folder per POI
**The separation already holds and World Design keeps it** (`10-structure.md`): `Framework/Runtime` (assembly `TileStories`,
ships), `Framework/Editor` (assembly `TileStories.Editor`, never ships), `Apps/<Wall>/` self-contained per wall, and the
Framework never references an `Apps/` folder. World Design adds: runtime components (Outline, Region, Spotlight, World
Moment, the spawner) and their shaders in `Framework/Runtime`; the spline <-> JSON sync, folder automation, import settings
and validation in `Framework/Editor`; every wall's world content (prefabs, timelines, PSB, video) in `Apps/<Wall>/`. A wall
prefab may use Framework components; never the reverse.

**JSON keys pointing at assets is already the pattern**: card media are paths relative to `card_settings.media_resources_path`
(`images/castle_hero.jpg` under `Apps/LivingRoom/Resources/LivingRoom/CardMedia/`), Framework defaults are `default:<key>`
(`CardMediaLibrary`), marker icons come from `MarkerSymbols/...`, all loaded through the `IMediaSource` seam. World content
adds one more kind of file (a prefab), not a new mechanism.

**Today the wall's files are sorted by TYPE** (`images/`, `audio/`, `video/`, `models/`) and the POI is only a filename prefix
(`castle_*`, `paco_*`). For a heavy app with ~150 POIs per wall, sort by OWNER first, then by type:
```
Apps/<Wall>/
  config.json, <Wall>Scene.unity, Scripts/, Tests/
  Map/                       Immersal .bytes + the textured .glb (the wall reference: OUTSIDE Resources, never shipped)
  Source/                    raw originals (today MediaAssets/): PSD, full-size video, notes; never loaded, never shipped
  Resources/<Wall>/          everything the app loads at runtime (the <Wall> prefix stops name clashes between walls)
    Wall/                    shared by the whole wall: mascot, ambient world prefab, shared pictures, marker icons
      images/ audio/ video/ models/ world/
    POIs/
      <poi_id>/              ONE folder per POI, named by its stable id (renaming the POI's display name moves nothing)
        images/ audio/ video/ models/ world/
```
Three levels, looked up the same way: Framework defaults (`default:<key>`, shared by every wall) -> `Wall/` (shared by this
wall) -> `POIs/<poi_id>/` (one POI). Rule: an asset used by two or more POIs belongs in `Wall/`.

**What the POI Editor does on its own (the developer never manages folders by hand):**
- "Add media / world content" on a POI: pick a file anywhere on disk -> it is copied into `POIs/<poi_id>/<type>/` (the type
  from its extension, the existing `MediaKind`) and the relative path is written into the config. Folders are created on the
  first file, never empty in advance (git does not keep empty folders; Unity warns about orphaned folder `.meta` files).
- "Move to shared" when a second POI uses a file; delete a POI -> offer to delete its folder, with its size.
- Every move goes through `AssetDatabase.MoveAsset` and rewrites the config's paths in the same undo step. **Never move these
  files in Windows Explorer**: prefabs point at assets by GUID (the `.meta` file) and the config by path, both break.
- An `AssetPostprocessor` applies import settings by folder and type (texture max size + ASTC compression, video transcode
  with Keep Alpha on Android, audio compression), so a 4K photo dropped in never ships at full size.
- Validation + a **media report**: missing files, orphans (no POI references them), files outside the wall folder, size per
  POI and per type, files over a budget.

**Blind spots for a heavy app:**
- **Everything under any `Resources/` folder ships in every build, used or not**, and walls share one global Resources
  namespace. Fine for one test wall (37 MB today); not for three real walls with video. The `IMediaSource` seam was built so
  `Resources` can be swapped for **Addressables** (download per wall, update content without a new build) -- that is Stage 6
  packaging and the folder layout above maps 1:1 onto Addressables groups (one per wall, maybe one per heavy POI).
- Store limits: Google Play caps the base download (Play Asset Delivery beyond it); iOS warns users before large cellular
  downloads (verify the current numbers when packaging). Whether each wall is its own app or one app holds every wall changes
  this a lot -- an open question for Stage 6.
- Moving today's card media from by-type to per-POI is a one-time migration done through the Editor (no compatibility shim,
  rule 00). It touches the card domains' media paths, so it is planned as its own block, after the `_3.3` review and before
  world content arrives.

## 5. Tools: what others do (verified where marked)
- **Artivive** (alive, verified): the artist uploads the artwork photo as the trigger and only places / scales imported video,
  image, 3D and particle layers on it. The tool authors layout, not art.
- **Adobe Aero**: discontinued, data deleted Dec 2025 (verified). **8th Wall**: hosted platform closed 2026-02-28, engine
  open-sourced (verified). **Meta Spark**: closed Jan 2025.
- **Snap Lens Studio Custom Landmarkers**: content placed on a scanned 3D model of a building, the same idea as section 4.2
  (alive, verified) -- inspiration, not a tool for this project.
- Pattern everywhere: the AR tool owns placement, anchoring, triggers and timing; art comes from dedicated tools; presets with
  a few sliders; a closed list of triggers; preview over the target in the editor, final check on the phone.

## 6. Unity stack (Unity 6.3, URP 17.3, AR Foundation 6.3)
| Need | Use | Avoid / note |
|---|---|---|
| Lines and regions on the wall | `com.unity.splines` (Draw Splines Tool snaps to the mesh) | a private vertex editor |
| Fills, outlines, sweeps, spotlight | Shader Graph unlit transparent meshes built from the spline | URP Decals (extra depth / DBuffer cost on mobile) |
| Glow | fake it in the shader (soft cross-section, additive) | URP Bloom over the camera feed (blooms the real image, costs ms) |
| Animation and sequencing | Animation window / Animator, Timeline (`PlayableDirector`) | a private keyframe or step editor |
| Particles | built-in Particle System | VFX Graph (needs compute; little gain at these counts) |
| Cut-out animation | 2D Animation + PSD Importer on SpriteRenderers, or a vertex-shader sway | -- |
| Vector animation | Rive Unity runtime (official, URP, still 0.x -- spike before relying on it) | Lottie (third-party runtimes only) |
| Video with alpha | WebM VP8 with alpha, **transcoded with Keep Alpha on Android** (verified, Unity manual) | more than one video decoder at a time with the AR camera running |
| 3D | glTFast GLB (already installed); the wall mesh as a depth-only occluder | Gaussian splats (not viable on mid Android); AR environment depth (short range) |
| Sharing | NativeShare (MIT) share sheet; recording via VideoKit (paid) or OS screen recording | Unity Recorder (Editor only) |

Mid-range Android with AR + Immersal: aim for 30 fps; the real cost is **overdraw** of large transparent layers (keep them
tight to the region), not draw calls.

## 8. Blind spots to discuss
- **Rights.** The Alto de Santa Catarina mural is a 2023 work by a living artist: animating, recolouring or cutting it up
  touches his moral right to the integrity of the work (Portuguese Copyright Code). Ask in writing first. The Panorama is
  public domain, but the museum (closed for works) controls access, filming and on-site use. Photos taken from Commons keep
  their licence (CC-BY-SA = attribution in the app).
- **Honesty.** AI colourisation, AI video and reconstructions must be labelled "artistic reconstruction" in the app.
- **Wow vs learning.** Decorative motion can lower learning ("seductive details", Mayer). Two consequences: each effect
  should point at the content (spotlight, then-and-now) more than decorate; and the Stage 7 evaluation should measure it
  (UEQ-S stimulation vs the knowledge check), which is also a citable finding.
- **Arm fatigue and session length.** Visitors hold a phone up at a wall: moments must be short (2-5 s), skippable, and the
  wall must stay readable in between.
- **Nothing has been under a thumb yet** (`__Architect_Handoff.md` section 3). This domain is the most device-dependent of
  all: alignment, exposure, overdraw and heat can only be judged on a phone in front of a painting. Step 0 is not optional.
- **Performance and heat**: Immersal re-localises periodically and spikes the CPU; effects should pause or lighten while it
  does.
- **Scope.** Decided: step 0 now, code after the `_3.3` component review.

Follows `10-structure.md`: domain folders, Framework never names a wall, Runtime never references Editor, and section 1.5 keeps
`UI/World/` for the uGUI markers -- World Design draws meshes and shaders, not UI, so it gets its own runtime folder.
```
Framework/Runtime/WorldDesign/
  WorldDesignData.cs     config classes: wall world settings, a POI's world (presence, regions, moments, content path)

                              the Phase A World gallery definitions

                              validation and the media report
```

on each hierarchy level a default presence and selection moment (like effects today); on each POI `world` = presence override,
