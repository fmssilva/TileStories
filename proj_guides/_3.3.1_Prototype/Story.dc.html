THE PALACE ON THE WATER
Two hundred and fifty years by the river

For about 250 years the kings of Portugal lived by the river, in a palace that opened straight onto the Tagus. It is gone now, but the square that took its place still carries its name.lede

When King Manuel I found the old castle on the hill too cramped for a court that was growing rich on the sea trade, he moved down to the water's edge. The new palace stood beside the docks where ships from India and Brazil unloaded spices, sugar and gold, so the king could watch the fleet come in from his own windows.paragraph · drop cap

Two centuries of additionsheading

Later kings kept adding to it. In the 1580s, under Philip I, who ruled Portugal and Spain together, the river front received a new facade and a domed corner tower, the Torreão, that sailors saw long before they saw the city.paragraph

WHAT WAS INSIDE
  • A library of some 70,000 books
  • Paintings, maps and the royal archive
  • A chapel, offices and workshops
  • The Ópera do Tejo, next door

Inside were a famous library of tens of thousands of books, paintings and maps, and a royal opera house, the Ópera do Tejo, opened only eight months before the end.paragraph

One morning in Novemberheading

On the morning of 1 November 1755, All Saints' Day, an earthquake shook the palace, a wave came up the river and fire finished what was left. The library, the archive and almost everything inside were lost.paragraph

A square takes its placeheading

Lisbon was rebuilt on a strict new plan, and the Praça do Comércio was laid out where the palace had been. Many Lisboetas still call it by its old name, Terreiro do Paço: the palace yard.paragraph
