# Research: entertaining actions on the tour

Research for Guidance's third dimension (Fun), 2026-11-10. It asks what real tour guides do to keep a tour alive, what the
evidence says, and which of those Azu can really do, in a wow way, in the Lab and in Unity 6.3. It is research, not a decision:
the open questions are in section 7. It reads with `_3.8_Guidance_Study.md` (the model) and `_3.3` section 11 (what is built).

## 1. The short answer

1. **Guides do not entertain by adding jokes. They make the visitor do something small with the content.** Guess, look up,
   find, vote, catch the guide out, answer at the end. Every good trick below is a tiny action tied to what was just said.
2. **The strongest evidence is for questions.** A test promised and given (retrieval practice), a guess made before the answer
   (pretesting), and short questions between parts (interpolated tests) each improve attention and memory. A promised quiz is
   not a gimmick; it is the best-supported idea in this report.
3. **Breaks help only when they are active.** A plain pause does not stop the mind wandering; a question does. So the
   breather and the check-in should end with a choice the visitor makes, not just a drink.
4. **Humour helps the mood, trust and attention, not memory by itself.** It must be about the content, rare, and never at the
   visitor. Forced humour does not make people laugh more.
5. **What fails:** interrupting a sentence, repeating, a game that blocks the tour, patronising praise, promises not kept,
   and any play at a place of grief.
6. **For TileStories this means one new group: Fun › On the tour.** Its items run between steps (or, for games the visitor
   accepted, inside a step). The tour chooses them once at its start (a **tour plan**), so a promise ("at the end I'll ask
   you") is always kept. They have their own rhythm (about one beat per 60-90 s of narration), not Fun's visit cap.
7. **The wow comes from what only Azu has:** it knows each word's exact time (the forbidden-word game), it sees the wall
   (find it on the wall), it has two voices per language (a historical cameo), and it holds the card's quiz blocks (the
   finale quiz, already built in Unity).

## 2. What the research says

### 2.1 Guides and interpretation

- **Tilden (1957):** the aim of interpretation is provocation, not instruction; it relates to the visitor's own life; it
  presents a whole, not a part; and children need their own programme, not a diluted adult one.
  ([NPS Foundations curriculum](https://www.nps.gov/idp/interp/101/FoundationsCurriculum.pdf),
  [Tilden's principles](https://wikieducator.org/Adventure_Tourism/Activities/Tilden%27s_Principles_of_Interpretation),
  [Interpret Europe on interpretation for children](https://interpret-europe.net/home-news/2021/ie-webinar-interpretation-for-children-exploring-cornells-sharing-nature-in-relation-to-ie-quality-standards/))
- **Sam Ham, TORE:** good interpretation has a **T**heme, is **O**rganised, **R**elevant and **E**njoyable. Enjoyable is
  a condition for attention, not decoration; relevance is what provokes thinking.
  ([Thematic interpretation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thematic_interpretation),
  [tools for themes](https://engagingplaces.net/2018/03/20/resources-for-strengthening-interpretive-themes/))
- **Weiler & Black, tour guiding research:** the guide is an "experience broker" in four domains: physical access,
  understanding, encounters and empathy. A good guide manages the group's energy as much as the facts.
  ([Weiler & Black, reconceptualising the guide's role](https://researchportal.scu.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Enhancing-the-visitor-experience-reconceptualising-the/991012821729702368),
  [experience brokering paper](https://core.ac.uk/download/334800531.pdf))
- **Guide training (NAI's Certified Interpretive Guide, Interpret Europe):** themes, audience involvement, role-play,
  questions that provoke a response. ([NAI CIG](https://eepro.naaee.org/learning/certified-interpretive-guide-training),
  [Interpret Europe CIG](https://interpret-europe.net/training/ie-courses/certified-interpretive-guide-cig/)). I did not find
  Blue Badge training material online; the storytelling advice of a UK tourism body is similar: stories over dates, "bite
  size packages" ([Tourism NI](https://www.tourismni.com/business-guidance/sector/tourist-guiding/tourist-guiding-growing-your-business/igniting-the-spark-story-telling/)).
- **Practical tricks** ([Thinking Museum: 10 ways to make tours interactive](https://thinkingmuseum.com/2021/10/27/10-easy-ways-to-make-your-guided-tours-more-interactive/)):
  ask questions and **wait**; say at the start that the tour is interactive; read the group; make them curious ("what are
  you wondering about?"); props; vary activity (pose like the figure); small choices ("agency"). **Visual Thinking
  Strategies**: "What's going on here? What do you see that makes you say that? What more can we find?"
  ([VTS for families](https://vtshome.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/VTS-Resources-for-Families.pdf)).
- **Museum Hack** (the "renegade" tours): gossip and back-stories, games every few stops, a second voice that joins or swaps
  in mid-tour for energy ([Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/North-America-Travel/Museum-Hack-adds-fun-and-games-to-the-typical-art-tour)).
- **Children:** I spy (colours, shapes, textures) gives "a focus for looking harder"; See-Think-Wonder; stamps for a finished
  hunt ([NHB Singapore, Playtime at museums](https://www.nhb.gov.sg/-/media/nhb/files/education/resources-for-families/playtime-at-museums.pdf)).
  A speech-therapy listening game is the forbidden-word game for kids: add a brick each time you hear the target word
  ([EANI attention and listening activities](https://send.eani.org.uk/sites/default/files/2024-02/Attention%20and%20Listening%20Activities%20P1-P4.pdf)).
  For adults the same game is BBC Radio 4's *Just a Minute*: talk 60 s without hesitation, repetition or deviation; the others
  buzz when you slip ([Just a Minute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_a_Minute)).

### 2.2 Evidence: attention, pacing and memory

- **Testing effect:** a test after study beats re-study for memory after 2 days and a week (Roediger & Karpicke 2006,
  [paper](https://psychology.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/216/2019/03/Roediger-Karpicke-2006.pdf)). It works best
  with feedback (the right answer after a wrong one) and questions that are effortful but mostly answerable.
- **Interpolated tests:** short tests inside a 21-minute online lecture halved mind wandering, tripled note-taking and lowered
  anxiety about the final test. A plain break or re-study did not help: "the testing is the critical component" (Szpunar,
  Khan & Schacter 2013, [PNAS, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3631699/)).
- **Pretesting (guess first):** a question before the text improves memory of the answer, even when the first guess was wrong;
  the error and its correction capture attention (Richland, Kornell & Kao 2009,
  [paper](https://learninglab.uchicago.edu/Pre-Testing_files/RichlandKornellKao.pdf)).
- **Curiosity gap:** curiosity is highest when people almost know (Loewenstein 1994; summary in
  [Nautilus](https://nautil.us/curiosity-depends-on-what-you-already-know-235803)). A teaser works when the gap is small and
  closed soon.
- **Segmenting:** people learn more from learner-paced segments than from one continuous stream (Mayer;
  [Cambridge, Segmenting principle](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/multimedia-learning/segmenting-principle/37240877DDA0362355ADB39936027982)).
  The tour's steps already are segments; the beats sit at their seams.
- **Attention lapses are short and frequent**, and a question or demonstration lowers them, also in the part after it
  (Bunce, Flens & Neiles 2010, [paper](https://wikicap.ulb.be/images/5/57/Bunce_Flens_et_al_2010.pdf)). The "10-minute
  attention span" is a myth; the speaker matters more than the format (Bradbury 2016,
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28145268/)).
- **Museum fatigue:** attention falls over a visit from satiation and overload; value = benefit / cost of attending (Bitgood;
  [museum fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_fatigue),
  [attention-value model](https://www.informalscience.org/attention-value-model-museum-visitors)). A beat must cost almost
  nothing (one tap) to be worth it.
- **Wait time:** after a question, teachers wait under 1 s; 3 s or more gives longer, better answers (Rowe;
  [summary](https://castle.eiu.edu/~scienced/5660/options/Op-4-R-4.html)). Azu must wait after it asks.
- **Peak-end:** a visit is remembered by its peak and its end; tourism studies find the end shapes satisfaction
  ([festival study](https://www.koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO201321365239060.page?&lang=en)). So the tour's last 20 s
  deserve a designed finale.
- **Audio guide length:** vendors advise 1-2 min per stop (30 s - 1.5 min for children); this is practice, not research
  ([Guide-ID](https://www.guide-id.com/newsroom/how-to-increase-museum-engagement-with-audio-tips-and-tricks)).

### 2.3 Humour, games, and what fails

- **Humour in teaching:** appropriate, content-related humour improves the climate and trust; its effect on memory is mixed;
  overuse and disrespect cost credibility (Banas et al. 2011, summary in
  [Faculty Focus](https://www.facultyfocus.com/articles/effective-teaching-strategies/humor-in-the-classroom-40-years-of-research/)).
- **Humour on tours:** asking guides to add more humour did not raise amusement; the humour guides already had helped
  comfort, **concentration** and connection (Pabel & Pearce 2016, [JCU](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/47286/)). Lesson:
  natural, rare, in character.
- **Gamified museum apps:** a gamified question-asking app led to more group discussion than the plain version
  ([ASU](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/the-impact-of-a-gamified-mobile-question-asking-app-on-museum-vis));
  visitors remembered details tied to quiz answers two weeks later in a treasure-hunt app
  ([Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-99-8088-8_3)); gamification raised engagement in a handheld AR museum study
  ([Authorea](https://www.authorea.com/users/143863/articles/271406-how-gamification-motivates-an-experimental-study-of-the-museum-experience-using-mobile-augmented-reality));
  an AR heritage-site game drew positive feedback for playful, meaningful visits
  ([HeritageSite AR](https://scholar.xjtlu.edu.cn/en/publications/heritagesite-ar-design-and-evaluation-of-a-mobile-augmented-reali-2/)).
  A city heritage game found players split into enthusiasts, the interested, sceptics and cynics
  ([ACM JOCCH 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3297716)): **games must be optional**, because a part of every audience dislikes them.
- **Kahoot:** in an NTNU study, removing points roughly halved engagement and removing points and music more than halved it
  ([NTNU](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/2562794)). Feedback and a score matter; sound matters.
- **Voice games (Alexa):** turn-based, simple answers, several variants of every line ("the ear hates hearing the same thing
  twice"), wrong answers handled with the right answer and context, a reprompt when the player hesitates, sound effects
  ([Amazon, voice-first games](https://developer.amazon.com/blogs/alexa/post/fcc7bc05-8cad-4669-9ae9-9ad48de830f6/best-practices-for-building-voice-first-games-for-alexa)).
- **Accuracy:** "interpretation must not dilute historical accuracy for entertainment" (a museum ethics code quoted at
  [Rutgers, Busta Myth](https://march-archives.camden.rutgers.edu/busta-myth)). Myth-or-fact must say clearly which is which.
- **Solemn places:** dark-tourism research treats "edutainment" as fit only for "lighter" dark sites
  ([Wyatt](https://napier-repository.worktribe.com/output/2455103)). Our fixed gate (no Fun on a Solemn point) is right.
- **Already in our plans:** Clippy and Navi (interrupting and repeating kill goodwill), TipKit (retire what is done), Duolingo
  (never the same line twice). They hold here too.

### 2.4 Examples that do it well

- **Smartify:** quizzes (single and multiple choice, images, a "try again" mode), Rubaway (swipe to reveal), Find the Detail,
  a memory game; inside the audio player, for family trails
  ([Smartify games](https://smartify.org/partners/articles/turn-curiosity-into-play-with-games-and-quizzes-from-smartify)).
- **MONA's The O:** Love / Hate on each work, then "where your ranking falls relative to everyone else's"
  ([Cooper Hewitt Labs](https://labs.cooperhewitt.org/2012/minutiae-future-now)).
- **Janet Cardiff's audio walks:** the voice tells you where to look and walk, binaural sound puts the past around you
  ([Cardiff & Miller walks](https://cardiffmiller.com/walks/)). The model for "look up" and "close your eyes".
- **Museum Hack:** games, gossip, a second voice. **Kahoot:** countdown, music, a score. **Alexa trivia:** variants and
  graceful wrong answers.

## 3. How it fits our model (a proposal)

### 3.1 Where it lives

- **A new group, Fun › On the tour.** Water break and Cookie (today's Moments) move into it. It inherits Fun's fixed gate
  (never on a Solemn point), Liveliness (Calm allows only the items marked *Calm too*) and the audiences' presets.
- **Its budget is the tour's, not the visit's.** Fun's visit cap (4, gap 300 s) would allow one beat per tour at best. On the
  tour, the rule is a **rhythm**: at most one beat per **75 s** of narration (60-120 s), never two seams in a row, at most
  **3 a tour** (Short 2, Deep 5), the opener and the finale included. The visit's Fun cap does not count them, and they do not
  delay the visit's tips (which are quiet in a tour anyway).
- **The tour plan.** When a tour starts, the tour asks the scheduler once for its plan: which opener, which middle beats at
  which seams, which finale. The plan is fixed (deterministic, so "Why not now?" can answer), and only what the visitor does
  changes it (a "no thanks", a stop, a skip). This is what makes a promise honest: the quiz is promised only if the plan holds
  it and the content has its questions.
- **Pairs.** A promise and its payoff are one pair (the quiz promise and the finale quiz; a teaser and its payoff). One never
  runs without the other. If the tour stops early, the payoff is offered on Resume or quietly dropped with one line
  ("We'll save the quiz for next time.").

### 3.2 New fields on an item (beside today's)

| Field | Values (default) |
|---|---|
| **Place in the tour** | Opening (after the introduction) / Between steps / **In a step** (on a word, for accepted games) / Finale (after the last step) |
| **The visitor answers by** | Nothing / Tap Azu / Chips (2-3) / Slider / Swipe / Tap the picture / Aim at the wall / Talk or type |
| **Waits** | 3-12 s (**8**, today's Moments) after it asks; then it carries on by itself ("No problem. Let's keep going.") |
| **Asks first** | **Yes** (games: "Let's play" / "Not now" chips) / No (a breather, a teaser) |
| **Needs** | what the point must hold: quiz questions, a year, a forbidden word, spots, a wall point, a cameo line. Missing → a named warning ("The quiz needs 3 questions; this point has 1.") |
| **Pairs with** | an item (promise ↔ payoff, teaser ↔ payoff) |
| **Scores** | Off / On (a count shown at the end) |
| **Calm too** | No / Yes (allowed when Liveliness is Calm) |
| **Audiences** | chips: Family / Explorer / Expert (today's Only if) |
| **Tour length** | Every length / Not on Short / Deep only (the steps' own rule) |

Today's fields keep their meaning: Says (1-4 variants, never the same twice in a row), Spoken (On the tour items are
**spoken**, since the tour's voice is already on), Mood, the prop, Importance, Shows (Once a tour / Once a visit / Once on each
card), After a dismissal (Not again this visit after **two** "Not now"s: the visitor does not want games).

### 3.3 Per point (Specific Marker › Guide script)

Per step, optional, EN / PT, each with **Draft with AI** and a **Reviewed** tick (as the audiences' drafts): a **quiz question**
(question, 2-3 answers, the right one, a "why" line of 12 words or fewer), a **guess** (a year or a number, its range), a
**myth or fact** line (and which it is), a **teaser** and its payoff step, a **callback** to an earlier step, a **cameo line**
(who speaks), **spots** for I spy (the step's existing spots), a **wall point** to find. Per point: the **forbidden word**
(suggested per language, see 5.4).

### 3.4 The visitor's controls

- Every beat has its way out: "Not now", a swipe, or simply waiting (8 s). No beat ever blocks the tour.
- Settings › The guide › **Games on the tour:** **On** / Off. The ✦ menu in a tour: **Play a game** (pull: the visitor
  starts one of the point's games at the next seam).
- The quiz's best score is kept per point (on the phone only, as the rest of the memory).

### 3.5 Fixed gates for this group

Never over a sentence (In a step games act between words, never stop them, see 5.4); never on a Solemn point; never with a
screen reader on unless the item has a button path; never two beats in a row; never the same item twice in a tour; never a
beat in the tour's first 20 s (except the Opening) or its last step's middle.

## 4. The ideas (21)

Each idea: what real guides do; how Azu does it; when it fits; the risk and its rule; the model; the Lab and Unity 6.3 paths;
the Editor rows. "Seam" means the gap between two steps. Every Editor row gets its demo (the developer's rule): the row shows
the beat on the phone, in a tour, now.

### 4.1 The breather (developer's example 1)
- **Real guides:** stop, drink, let the group catch up; pauses help thinking (Thinking Museum, "Pause"); a break alone does
  not restore attention, a question does (Szpunar 2013).
- **Azu:** "Enjoying it so far? Let me catch my breath… *(drinks)* Uff. Shall we go on?" Azu walks to the middle with the
  glass (today's prop), drinks (a drink clip), wipes its mouth. Chips: **Go on** / **A question first**. Waiting 8 s = Go on.
- **When:** Between steps, after 60 s of narration (today), at a paragraph's end; once a tour; Lively; Explorer and Family.
- **Risk → rule:** cute the first time, irritating the third → once a visit by default (Shows: Once a visit), variants
  (water, a fan in summer, a scarf in winter: the Seasonal dates). It ends with a choice, so it is an active break.
- **Model:** today's Water break, moved to Fun › On the tour; Place Between steps; Answers Chips; Calm too No.
- **Lab:** today's Moment plus the chips. **Unity:** Animator "drink" clip on the GLB (or a Rive state); the glass a sprite in
  Azu's hand bone's screen point; chips are UI Toolkit Buttons in the dock.
- **Editor rows:** After narration (60 s) · Says · Prop (glass / fan / scarf by date) · Chips · Shows.

### 4.2 The check-in that steers (developer's example 2)
- **Real guides:** read the group and adapt; ask what they wonder about; invite questions (Thinking Museum 1, 3, 5).
- **Azu:** "Enjoying it so far? Any question, just ask me here." Azu points at the talk button (a ring on it). Chips:
  **Keep going** / **Shorter, please** / **Tell me more**. Shorter switches the rest of the tour to Short (the steps "Not on
  Short" drop out, the progress line shrinks with a glide); Tell me more switches to Deep. "Got it."
- **When:** once a tour, about halfway (the seam nearest 50 %), not on tours under 90 s; skipped if the visitor asked a
  question or played in the last 60 s (they are clearly with us).
- **Risk → rule:** a check-in nobody answers is noise → it waits 5 s with the talk button lit, then "Let's go on." The Length
  change is said back and undoable ("Back to the full tour" chip in the dock for 5 s).
- **Model:** Fun › On the tour (also fits App › The guide, since it teaches Ask); Teaches: Ask; Calm too Yes.
- **Lab:** the tour's step list is already filtered by Length; re-filter the remaining steps. **Unity:** the same filter on
  the tour's step list; the segmented progress line redrawn (`generateVisualContent`), widths tweened.
- **Editor rows:** Where (Halfway / after n steps) · Says · Chips shown (Keep going, Shorter, More) · Skip when the visitor
  just asked (Yes).

### 4.3 The promised quiz (developer's example 3; worked out in 5.3)
- **Real guides:** "I'll ask you at the end, pay attention." Evidence: the testing effect, interpolated tests, test feedback.
- **Azu:** opener: "Just so you know: at the end I'll ask you three questions. Pay attention ;)" A small star appears at the
  end of the progress line. Finale: three questions in the card's quiz look; tap or say the answer; right / wrong with the
  why; a score; the best score kept.
- **When:** Opening + Finale (a pair); tours of 4 steps or more; Family and Explorer (Expert: opt-in, harder questions);
  never Solemn.
- **Risk → rule:** a quiz on what was skipped is unfair → only questions from steps heard (80 % played); a quiz feels like
  school → "Not now" at the promise, "Skip the quiz" at the finale, no timer, no buzzer, wrong answers get the why kindly.
- **Model:** two items, Pairs with each other; Needs 3 quiz questions per language; Scores On.
- **Lab / Unity:** see 5.3. The quiz block (multiple_choice, image_choice, true_false_swipe) is already built in Unity (8A).
- **Editor rows:** Questions (3) · From (each third of the tour) · Answers by (Tap / Tap or say) · Show me after a wrong
  answer (On) · Promise line · Finale lines by score.

### 4.4 Don't say it! (developer's example 4; worked out in 5.4)
- **Real guides:** a game that makes people listen for something; *Just a Minute*'s buzz; the children's listening game.
- **Azu:** "Just for fun: in this part I'll try not to say *palace*. If I do, tap me!" During the step, each time the word is
  said, a tap on Azu in time counts; Azu flinches and a "Caught 1" pip shows. At the end: "I said it three times. You caught
  two!" and, for a miss, Azu plays its own slip back: "Listen: '…the palace burned…' Gotcha."
- **When:** In a step (accepted with "Let's play"); one game a tour; Family and Explorer; Lively; never Solemn.
- **Risk → rule:** it could make people listen for one word and not the story → only in one step or two, the word chosen
  among the story's key words (so listening for it means listening to the story).
- **Model:** In a step; Answers Tap Azu; Needs a forbidden word with 2-4 occurrences in the span; Scores On.
- **Lab / Unity:** see 5.4. The recorded sets already hold every word's start and end.
- **Editor rows:** The word (suggested) · The span (steps) · Tap window (2.5 s) · False taps (a playful "Nope!") · Play my
  slip back (On).

### 4.5 Guess the year
- **Real guides:** "How old do you think this is?" before they say it. Evidence: pretesting; the curiosity gap is largest
  when people almost know.
- **Azu:** "Before I tell you: how old do you think this panel is?" A year slider appears in the dock (1400 → 1900, thumb at
  the middle). The visitor drags and lets go, or taps **Tell me**. Then the step says the year, and Azu reacts to the
  distance: "Only 20 years off!" / "Older than you'd think!". The guess stays as a pin on the slider, the answer as a second.
- **When:** Between steps, before the step that holds the year; once a tour; every audience (Expert: a tighter range); Calm
  too.
- **Risk → rule:** a guess with no clue is a lottery → the range is set by the author so a good guess is possible (the
  curiosity gap is small); the reaction never mocks.
- **Model:** Fun › On the tour; Answers Slider; Needs a guess (a number and its range) on the next step; pairs into the
  callback (4.10) and the finale ("You guessed 1650. It was 1700.").
- **Lab:** an input range styled as the Before / After handle. **Unity:** a UI Toolkit `SliderInt` with a custom drag handle,
  or the Before / After drag's own PointerManipulator; the two pins are elements placed by value.
- **Editor rows:** The number (year) · Range · Unit (year / metres / tiles / people) · Reactions by distance (3 bands).

### 4.6 Myth or fact
- **Real guides:** "Mythbusting" walks; tell a famous legend, ask if it is true. Accuracy rule: always say which is which.
- **Azu:** "Myth or fact? The king watched the earthquake from this window." A statement card in the dock: swipe right for
  Fact, left for Myth (the card's true_false_swipe). Then: "Myth! He was in Belém that morning." with a source line.
- **When:** Between steps; once a tour; Explorer and Expert (Family gets I spy instead); Calm too.
- **Risk → rule:** a myth said aloud can be remembered as fact → the verdict is spoken and written at once, and the myth is
  never left standing at the tour's end.
- **Model:** Answers Swipe; Needs a myth-or-fact line per step.
- **Lab:** the card's true / false swipe component in the dock. **Unity:** the existing true_false_swipe (`SwipeGrabRule`, the
  tick / cross glyphs) hosted in the dock's sheet.
- **Editor rows:** Statement · It is (Myth / Fact) · Verdict line · Source shown (Yes).

### 4.7 Find it on the wall (the AR wow)
- **Real guides:** "Look up. Do you see the ship, top left?" Tilden: the real thing first. Cardiff's walks: the voice tells
  you where to look.
- **Azu:** "Lower the card and find the ship on the real wall. Point me at it." The card slides to Mini, the camera view
  comes back with a soft "seek" ring in the middle. When the wall point settles in the middle (today's event, 1.2 s still),
  Azu: "That's it!" The point lights, a short glint. **I give up** chip shows it (Azu walks to it).
- **When:** Between steps, on the wall only (the tour of a card whose point is on this wall, the wall tracked); once a tour;
  every audience; Calm too.
- **Risk → rule:** the arm tires and the wall may be lost → 20 s at most, then Azu shows it; never when tracking is lost
  (system states win); never near a street (Care's "Look up near the street").
- **Model:** Answers Aim at the wall; Needs a wall point; Teaches: Ask about the view (the same gesture).
- **Lab:** the Lab's wall drag stands in for the phone; "settled in the middle" exists. **Unity:** the wall point's anchor
  through the AR camera's `WorldToScreenPoint`, distance to the screen's centre under a radius for 1.2 s; the card sheet to
  Mini; the ring a Painter2D arc.
- **Editor rows:** The point · Says · Give up after (20 s) · Hint after (10 s: "A little to the left…", from the point's
  screen direction).

### 4.8 I spy in the picture (Family)
- **Real guides:** I spy with colours, shapes, textures; Smartify's Find the Detail.
- **Azu:** "I spy with my little eye… something with a tail!" The step's picture fills the block; the visitor taps it. Wrong:
  "Look again!" and the light warms towards it after 2 tries. Right: the spot zooms (today's Into pictures) and the step goes
  on about it.
- **When:** Between steps, on a step with a picture and spots; once a tour; Family (Explorer opt-in); Calm too.
- **Risk → rule:** a tiny target is frustrating → spots at least 44 px on the phone at the picture's size; after 3 misses,
  it shows it.
- **Model:** Answers Tap the picture; Needs a spot with an I spy clue.
- **Lab:** the spots of the Guide script, hit-tested on the picture's box. **Unity:** a `PointerDownEvent` on the image
  element, the point mapped to u, v; the zoom is today's frame + scale + translate.
- **Editor rows:** Clue (per language) · Spot · Hints after (2 misses) · Show after (3).

### 4.9 The cliffhanger
- **Real guides:** "But that's not the end of the story… I'll tell you upstairs." Curiosity gap.
- **Azu:** at a step's end: "And then the king did something no one expected. You'll see in a moment." Mood: a sly wink.
  The payoff step opens with "So, what did the king do?"
- **When:** a seam with an authored payoff 1-2 steps later; at most 1 a tour; every audience; Calm too.
- **Risk → rule:** a teaser not paid off breaks trust → Pairs with its payoff; if the visitor stops or the payoff step is
  hidden by Length or the lens, the teaser is not said.
- **Model:** Says on a step's end; Pairs with a step. It is authored content, so it may belong to Content (open question).
- **Lab / Unity:** a line inserted at the seam; no new UI.
- **Editor rows:** Teaser line · Paid off at (step) · A warning when the payoff step is hidden for an audience.

### 4.10 The callback
- **Real guides and comedians:** refer back to what the group saw or said; it ties the tour together (Tilden: a whole).
- **Azu:** "Remember the crack from 1755 in the first tile? Here it is again, on the tower." Or to the visitor's own act:
  "You guessed 1650. It was 1700: you were close."
- **When:** a seam whose step has a callback, only if the earlier step was heard (or the guess was made); at most 2 a tour.
- **Risk → rule:** "remember…?" about something unheard is confusing → only callbacks to what this visitor heard or did.
- **Model:** a step's field (Callback to) and slots `{guess}` `{answer}` `{caught}` filled from the tour plan's memory.
- **Lab / Unity:** a line choice at the seam; the slots from the tour's memory.
- **Editor rows:** Callback to (step) · Line · Or to the visitor's guess (Yes).

### 4.11 What would you do? (a poll)
- **Real guides:** "Who here would have stayed in Lisbon after the earthquake?" A show of hands. MONA's O shows where your vote

- **Azu:** "Would you rather live in the palace or by the river?" Two chips. Then: "62 % chose the river. Me too: fresher
  sardines." Offline: Azu's own pick only.
- **When:** Between steps; once a tour; Explorer and Family; Calm too.
- **Risk → rule:** counts from a handful of votes mislead → show a share only after 30 votes; never ask about personal data.
- **Model:** Answers Chips; Needs two options; the shared count is a service.
- **Lab:** the published Lab's shared data (as the Voice test's scores). **Unity:** the token service / Worker keeps counts per
  question; offline, Azu's line only.
- **Editor rows:** Question · Two options · Azu's own pick and line · Show others' choices (after 30 votes).

### 4.12 The cameo voice
- **Real guides:** a second voice or a guide who acts a character (Museum Hack; NAI's role-play).

- **When:** In a step's text or at a seam; at most 1 a tour; every audience; Calm too (the cameo is content).
- **Risk → rule:** invented quotes damage accuracy → the cameo is marked "as he might have said" unless it is a real quote with
  a source.
- **Model:** a step's Cameo line (speaker, words, real quote Yes / No); the voice set has two voices per language already.
- **Lab:** the recorded line in the second voice (voicebench render); the live voice falls back to the other provider voice.
  **Unity:** an AudioSource clip from the second voice; a prop sprite on the head bone; the mouth from that line's visemes.
- **Editor rows:** Speaker · Line · Real quote (source) / As he might have said · Voice (the other voice of the set).

### 4.13 Close your eyes and imagine
- **Real guides:** "Close your eyes. It's 1750. You hear…" Cardiff's binaural walks.
- **Azu:** "Close your eyes for ten seconds. Listen." The screen dims to the wall, the music bed is replaced by a soundscape
  (the market, gulls, bells, the river) for 8-10 s, Azu's voice soft over it. A tap anywhere ends it.
- **When:** Between steps, at a step about daily life; once a visit; Explorer and Expert; Calm too; on Solemn points only as a
  **thoughtful** variant (a silence, no sounds), if the developer allows (open question).
- **Risk → rule:** eyes closed in a street is unsafe → never when Care says the visitor is near a street or moving; "keep
  your feet still" in the line; it needs sound, so it is offered only when the sound is on (not muted).
- **Model:** Fun › On the tour; Needs a soundscape clip.
- **Lab:** Web Audio, a clip with a fade, the voice ducked as the music bed already is. **Unity:** an AudioSource for the bed
  with an AudioMixer snapshot to duck; the dim a full-screen element with opacity.
- **Editor rows:** Soundscape · Length (8 s) · Says · Only with sound on (Yes).

### 4.14 The prop from the pocket
- **Real guides:** pass around a tile, a coin, a spice (Thinking Museum: props).
- **Azu:** "I brought something." Azu pulls a small picture from behind its back (a coin of D. João V, a quill, a tile

- **When:** Between steps or on a word in the step; at most 1 a tour; every audience.
- **Risk → rule:** clutter → the prop is one of the point's own images; it leaves when the step goes on.
- **Model:** Needs a prop image and a line; Answers Tap (optional).
- **Lab:** an image element animated from Azu's hand to the middle. **Unity:** a VisualElement with a translate and scale
  tween from the hand bone's screen point; the flip is today's flip card.
- **Editor rows:** Prop image · Front line · Back line · On the word (optional).

### 4.15 You choose what's next
- **Real guides:** small choices give ownership (Thinking Museum: agency).

- **When:** a seam where the next two steps are independent (the author marks them "can swap"); once a tour; Explorer.

## 5. The developer's four examples, worked out

### 5.1 The breather

- **Trigger:** the first seam after 60 s of narration that ends a paragraph, if the tour plan holds the breather and no other
  beat came in the last 75 s.
- **Flow:**
  1. The step's last word ends. Azu turns to the visitor (smile).
  2. "Enjoying it so far? Let me just catch my breath…" / "Está a gostar até agora? Deixe-me só recuperar o fôlego…"
  3. Azu walks to the middle (0.6 s), lifts the glass, drinks (1.2 s, a gulp sound only if sounds are On), wipes.
  4. "Uff. OK, shall we go on?" / "Ufa! Vamos continuar?" Chips: **Go on** / **A question first**.
  5. Go on, or 8 s with nothing → "Let's continue." and the next step. A question first → the dock rises to Peek, the talk
     button listens; after the answer, "Back to our story." and the next step.
  6. A poke on Azu while it drinks → it splutters (a Lively reaction), then goes on.
- **Variants:** a fan (June to September), a scarf (December to February), a coffee in the morning (before 11:00). Never the
  same prop twice in a visit.
- **Never:** Calm, Solemn, a tour under 90 s, the tour's last seam.

### 5.2 The check-in

- **Trigger:** the seam nearest the tour's middle (40-60 % of its narration), once a tour, if the visitor has not asked or
  played in the last 60 s.
- **Flow:**
  1. "Are you enjoying it so far? If you have any question, just ask me here." / "Está a gostar? Se tiver alguma pergunta,
     é só perguntar aqui." Azu points at the talk button; one ring.
  2. Chips: **Keep going** / **Shorter, please** / **Tell me more**. The talk button is live (a question goes to Ask Azu).
  3. Keep going or 5 s → "…and let's continue." / "…e vamos continuar."
  4. Shorter → "Sure, the short version." The remaining steps marked Not on Short drop; the progress line shrinks; a "Back to
     the full tour" chip stays 5 s. Tell me more → "Happy to." Deep steps join.
  5. A question → the answer, then "Back to our story."
- **Learning:** the choice is remembered for the visit: Shorter sets the next tours to Short (said once: "I'll keep the next
  ones short too.").

### 5.3 The promised quiz

**The promise (Opening):**
1. After the introduction, before step 1: "Just so you know: at the end I'll ask you three questions. Pay attention ;)" /
   "Só para saber: no fim faço-lhe três perguntas. Esteja atento ;)". Azu winks.
2. A star appears at the end of the dock's progress line (it says "Quiz" under it at Peek). Chips for 4 s: **Deal** / **No
   quiz, thanks** (No: the star goes, the finale becomes 4.18).
3. Made only if: the tour has 4 or more steps; the point has at least 3 reviewed questions in this language, from at least 2
   different steps; not Solemn; Games on the tour On; the audience allows it (Expert: "Want three tough questions at the
   end?" instead).

**During the tour:** nothing is said about it again, except once, before the step a question comes from, at most once: "This
one's on the quiz…" (Family only; optional, the developer to judge).

**Choosing the questions (at the last seam):**
- Only questions from steps heard (80 % of the step played, at any speed; a skipped step does not count).
- One from each third of the tour when possible; never two from one step; not a question already used in a Myth or fact.
- If fewer than 2 qualify: "We went fast! I'll save the quiz for next time." The star fades. (Promise kept honestly.)
- Family: image_choice and true / false first; Explorer: multiple choice; Expert: the hardest-marked.

**The finale:**
1. "Time for my questions!" Azu takes out a quiz card (a prop), the dock rises to Peek.
2. Question 1 of 3, in the card's quiz look (the question as plain text, 2-3 choices as buttons with the tick / cross glyphs).
   The question is spoken; the choices are read only if the visitor waits 3 s (wait time).
3. The visitor taps a choice, or says it ("the second", "1755", "the palace": matched against the choices' words in the
   language; speech is an extra path, never the only one), or taps **Not sure**.
4. Right: a tick, a light haptic (the visitor's own action), Azu delighted, "Yes!" (4 variants). Wrong: the cross on theirs,
   the tick on the right one, "Close. It was {answer}. {why}" and a **Show me** chip that goes to the block (the Go to tool).
   Not sure: the right one shows, "It was {answer}. {why}".
5. Next question after the line ends (or a tap). No timer.
6. **The score:** 3 of 3: "Three out of three! You'd make a fine guide." The stamp turns gold (4.19). 2 of 3: "Two out of
   three. Very good." 1: "One out of three. The tiles are tricky!" 0: "Zero… but now you know three new things!" (never
   mocking; a zero still ends on a fact).
7. **The end:** one line of the finale in three is folded in ("The thing to remember: …"), then the offer of what is next.
8. **Skip the quiz** is on the dock during the finale; it ends with the plain finale.

**Stops and resumes:** a stop keeps the plan; Resume before the end keeps the promise. If the visitor stops for good, the ✦
menu shows **Your 3 questions** for the rest of the visit (pull), from the steps heard.

**Memory:** best score per point; "Quiz: 2/3" on the card's guide block; never sent.

**Lab:** `guidance` On the tour items; the tour's step-end hook asks for the finale; the quiz rendered with the Lab's card
quiz components in the dock; speech matching on the existing recogniser's text. **Unity:** the knowledge-check views of 8A
(`Choice.Mark`, `VerdictMark`, `CardIcons.VectorGlyph`) hosted in the dock's sheet; the question's audio a recorded line;
speech from the platform recogniser → text → the same matcher (plain C#, EditMode-tested on the Lab's cases).

**Tests the developer can press (Ask Azu's scenes):** A promised quiz, all right · Two wrong, Show me · A stop, then the quiz
from the ✦ · A skipped step: no question from it · Too few questions: no promise · Expert: the opt-in.

### 5.4 Don't say it! (the forbidden-word game)

**Choosing the word (Editor, per point, per language):**
- The Editor suggests words from the tour's text that appear **2-4 times** in a span of 1-2 consecutive steps (45-120 s), not in
  the span's first sentence, 4 letters or more, a noun the story is about (a name, a building, an object), and says each one's
  count and span: "palace: 3 times in steps 2-3 (70 s)". In Portuguese the same game may need another word ("palácio": 3 in
  steps 2-3). Each language is checked on its own; a language without a fitting word does not offer the game.
- The author picks one per language. A warning if the count is 1 (no game) or over 4 (too easy, too long).

**The flow:**
1. **Announce (the seam before the span):** "Just for fun: in this part I'll try not to say *palace*. If I say it, tap me so I
   know you're paying attention!" / "Só por diversão: nesta parte vou tentar não dizer *palácio*. Se eu disser, toque-me, para
   eu saber que está atento!" The word is shown on a small card by Azu ("palace" with a crossed speech bubble), which stays
   pinned beside Azu during the span. Chips 4 s: **Let's play** / **Not now**. Waiting = Let's play (the developer's example
   just starts; open question).
2. **The span plays normally.** The narration is the step's own text; the occurrences of the word **are** the slips. (Optional:
   one authored near miss, "the pal… the royal house", as an extra recorded line.)
3. **Each slip:** the word's start and end come from the recorded word times (the live voice's times; the device voice's
   estimate). The **tap window** is the word's start to its end + **2.5 s** (outdoors, a phone in hand; tune with the five
   visitors).
4. **A tap on Azu in a window = caught.** At once, without stopping the voice: Azu flinches and blushes, a "Caught 1" pip
   by the word card, a light haptic. At the next sentence's end, a 2-word aside if the pause allows ("Caught me!" / "Apanhou-me!",
   one of 4, recorded), else nothing more. One tap per slip counts; more taps in the same window are ignored.
5. **A tap outside every window = a false alarm.** Azu shakes its head (a tiny "Nope!" bubble for 1 s, no voice). Counted, never
   punished.
6. **Pokes are off during the span** (a tap on Azu means "you said it"). Three quick taps still tickle (a giggle, no count).
7. **End of the span (the next seam):** the result line, with the miss played back:
   - all caught, no false alarms: "Three times, and you caught me every time. Sharp ears!"
   - some caught: "I said it three times. You caught two!" then, for the first miss: "Here's the one you missed:" and the
     recorded clip of that moment plays (from 1.2 s before the word to 0.3 s after), the word lit in the bubble: "…the
     **palace** burned in 1755… Gotcha!"
   - none caught: "Hey, I said it three times and you didn't tap once!" then one slip played back, and "Were you dreaming of
     pastéis de nata?"
   - many false alarms (3 or more): "And I didn't say it {n} times you tapped. Nice try!"
   - With the live or device voice (no recorded clip), the slip's phrase is spoken again instead.
8. **Score kept:** caught / slips, said once more in the finale's callback ("…and you caught me saying *palace* twice").

**Edge cases:**
- **Pause, a drag, Ask:** the windows freeze with the voice; Resume re-arms them where they were.
- **Previous (the sentence again):** a slip already counted is not counted twice; a missed one can be caught on the replay
  (generous).
- **Dock at Compact:** Azu sits by the ✦, and a tap there opens the dock; so during the span a **"Heard it!"** button shows by
  the ✦ instead. **Mascot Off:** the same button. **Screen reader:** the game is not offered. **Reduce Motion:** no flinch,
  the pip only.
- **Language changed mid-span, or the step hidden by Length or a lens:** the game ends quietly ("Let's play another time.").
- **Captions:** the word lights like every word (no special mark: that would give it away; the captions help the hard of
  hearing play too).

**Why it works:** it rewards listening to the story itself; the slips are real, so it needs no extra content; the play-back
of its own slip is something only Azu can do (it knows the word's time in its own recording).

**Lab:** the tour's word-time events (they already drive the highlight) open and close the windows; the tap on Azu goes to the
game while it runs; the slip clip is the recorded audio element seeked to the window. **Unity:** the AudioSource's
`time` against the recorded word times; a `PointerManipulator` on the Azu element routed to the game during the span; the
clip played back by `PlayScheduled` from the window's start with a stop after its end; the pip a UI Toolkit label with a USS
scale transition; the blush a morph or a Rive input.

**Tests the developer can press:** Catch every slip · Miss one: hear it back · Tap too early (a false alarm) · Pause in a
window · Compact dock: the Heard it! button · Portuguese with its own word.
