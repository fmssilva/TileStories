# Audiences & Personalisation -- study (2026-11-02)

Who the visitor is (a family, a curious tourist, an expert) and what they want (short or deep, a topic) changes what each block
says, how the Guide tells it and how the card looks. This is a study for its own design domain: it records the options and a
recommendation, and it is not a build plan yet. Plan it with `skills/Domain_Planning.md` after `_3.3` closes.

Its scope:
- the audience presets;
- the length, the topic lenses;
- per-block content variants with fallback;
- the audience style tokens;
- the Guide's targets per audience;
- the AI-draft review rule;
- the privacy rules.

The other guides (`_3.3` card styles, `_3.4` Guide) refer to its tokens and rules instead of restating them, so every block kind
uses one mechanism.

Research: one read-only subagent (2026-11-02) + the Architect. **Status of the facts:** most sources are vendor pages and
secondary legal summaries. Items marked (unverified) were not confirmed against a primary source, and the privacy notes are not
legal advice.

## 1. What the developer asked for
- Visitor profiles (child, tourist, scientific historian) that change the length and the type of text, and maybe the blocks'
  look (cartoon cards for children, premium minimal for historians).
- Every block with one default content that all profiles share; content per profile only where the author wants it.
- An onboarding panel where the visitor picks a profile and how they want the information: compact or long, serious and factual
  or human and fun, and maybe other dimensions.
- Topic sub-domains chosen during the visit (São Jorge Castle: its military analysis, its political weight, its architecture).
- The Guide's behaviour and scripts follow the profile.
- Prototype it in this Lab, but maybe as a domain of its own.

## 2. What others do (2025-2026)
- **Fixed parallel tracks are still the norm.**
  - The Met: a kids' audio guide (13 tours, ages 6-12).
  - Bloomberg Connects museums: "Kids & Families" next to Highlights.
  - The 9/11 Museum: a family track narrated by a child.
  - Rijksmuseum: tours by theme.
- **Short quiz to a route.**
  - Smartify asks for interests and available time.
  - Locatify asks 3 questions (language, topics, time).
  - amuseapp uses a very short quiz.
  - In all of them the curators' stories stay the same; only the route changes.
- **artlas (Mori Art Museum and others):** an AI guide adapted to age, interests, language and time. It is built on Claude and
  supervised by the museum (it generates; the museum reviews and approves).
- **Newsela, the closest analogue:** the same article rewritten by editors at about 5 reading levels, with a quiz per level.
  Simple English Wikipedia's rule fits here: "simple does not mean short", so register and length are separate axes.
- **Dimensions offered in practice:** interests, time, language, age or family.
  - Depth and tone are almost never separate controls: they come inside a track ("Kids tour", "Director's tour").
- **Onboarding quizzes:** about 13-16 % of apps show one, usually with 2-4 questions; Skip is always offered (vendor analysis).
  - Headspace asks one question to set the length of the first sessions.
  - Netflix Kids is the example to avoid: a coarse profile that can only be changed by deleting it.
- **AI-drafted variants:** in LLM simplifications, the main errors are lost information and added material outside the source.
  Plain invented facts are rare (under 1 %), and detectors miss these errors (arXiv 2505.16392). The BBC's AI summaries and
  artlas both keep a human approval step.

## 3. Recommendation

### 3.1 The dimensions: three, only one asked
1. **Audience preset:** Family / **Explorer** (default) / Expert. It sets the tone, the depth, the style tokens and the Guide's
   persona. It is a content preference, never an age.
2. **Length:** Short / **Standard** / Deep. It defaults from the preset (Family -> Short, Expert -> Deep) and can be changed in
   settings.
3. **Lenses:** topics chosen during the visit, never at onboarding.

Language and accessibility (text size, Reduce Motion, captions) follow the phone, not the profile.

The developer's "serious and factual vs human and fun" is part of the preset, with no slider of its own:
- Family is playful;
- Explorer is warm and factual;
- Expert is sober and dense.

One more axis can come later, if visitors ask for it.

### 3.2 Onboarding: one screen, skippable
- **When:** the first time a card opens, with the camera-permission step and not before it. The visitor has just seen a wall
  and knows why they are being asked.
- **The screen:** "Who's exploring today?", with three large illustrated cards, each with a one-line promise.
  - Family: "Short stories, games and a playful guide".
  - Explorer: "The stories, told well".
  - Expert: "Dates, sources and the details".
- **Skip** is visible and means Explorer. There is no account, no age and no second screen.
- **Changing it later:**
  - the card's Settings: Audience and Length;
  - the Guide's "Simpler / Tell me more" at the end of a step (it moves Length one step).

### 3.3 Topic lenses
- **Where:** a chip row under the title, "See it as: Story · Military · Politics · Architecture". It shows only when the point
  has 2 or more blocks tagged with a topic.
- **What a chip does:** it filters and reorders the tagged blocks. The core blocks (no topic) always stay, and so does the
  scroll position on them.
- **Over the visit:** the last lens stays for the session and is offered at the next point that has it. The Guide plays the
  steps tagged with it.
- **Why:** multiple perspectives on one object are established museum practice. The Minneapolis Institute of Art shows
  several labels per object; Cleveland's Community Voices adds labels in visitors' own words, and its app carries the extra
  layers.

### 3.4 Content: one default, sparse overrides, fall back per field
```
Block {
  id, kind,
  content: Fields                                      // the default, shared by every audience
  overrides: { family?: FieldsPatch, expert?: FieldsPatch }  // per field, sparse
  visibleFor: Family | Explorer | Expert (all by default)   // e.g. a Family-only "spot the ship" quiz
  topics: string[]                                     // the lenses; empty = core, always shown
  overrideMeta[audience]: { source: human | aiDraft, reviewed, basedOn: the default's hash }
}
```
- **Resolve per field:** `overrides[audience].field ?? content.field`. Family never falls back to Expert, nor the other way
  round.
- **Stale:** when the default changes after an override was written (the hash differs), the override is marked stale until the
  author looks at it again.
- **An AI draft that is not reviewed never ships.**
- **The same mechanism for the Guide script:** a step's words have a default and per-audience overrides, and its steps carry
  topics.
- **Where to spend the authoring effort first:**
  - Family overrides for the Story, the Fun fact and the Quiz;
  - Expert overrides for the Story, the Timeline and the Sources.
  - Everything else inherits the default.

### 3.5 The look per audience: tokens, not a second design
- **One component set; each audience swaps a small token set:**
  - corner radius and type scale step;
  - the accent palette;
  - an illustration / sticker slot (on for Family);
  - the mascot's presence and liveliness;
  - the amplitude of motion.
- **Contrast and tap sizes** must pass for every set.
- **Expert:** the base look with the ornaments off, so no separate premium design is needed.
- **Avoid a cartoon skin:** YouTube Kids was redesigned in 2024 to look less toy-like, and a cartoon skin is only right for
  under-8s, who visit with a parent anyway.

### 3.6 The Guide per audience
A preset sets:
- the script's target length (to test: Family about 30-45 s, Explorer 60-90 s, Expert 2-3 min per card);
- the register;
- the voice style (speed, and an expressive style where the voice allows it);
- the mascot (Family Lively, adults Calm, `_3.4` section 4.4).

### 3.7 The Editor
- **Preview as:** Family / Explorer / Expert on the phone preview.
- **Coverage per point:** blocks x Default / Family / Expert. Each cell reads Inherited / Overridden / Hidden / Stale / AI draft
  to review, with totals per point and per wall. Warnings name the block and the point, never ids.
- **On an open block:**
  - an "Override for Family" next to each field, and "Back to the default";
  - **Draft with AI**:
    - it shows the draft side by side with the default;
    - it flags every name, date and number that is not in the default;
    - it needs the author's Reviewed tick before it ships.
- **Lenses:**
  - a wall-level list of topics (name per language, icon);
  - a Topics field on each block and on each Guide step.

### 3.8 Privacy (not legal advice; check with a lawyer before launch)
- **The rules that apply:**
  - Portugal sets the digital consent age at 13 (GDPR Art. 8, Lei 58/2019 Art. 16).
  - The UK Children's Code applies to an app children are likely to use, which an AR heritage app is. It requires high-privacy
    defaults, profiling and location off by default, and no nudging.
  - COPPA (as amended, enforceable since 22 April 2026) and the Apple Kids Category / Google Families rules apply when the app
    targets children.
- **What follows for the profile:**
  - It is a content preference ("Family"), never an age. Never ask for an age.
  - It is stored on the phone only, never sent to analytics, and never inferred from behaviour (inferring it would be
    profiling).
  - The app is not marketed "for kids" and is declared for a general audience.

## 4. Risks
- **Authoring cost:** 3 audiences x N blocks x 2 languages. Answer: sparse overrides, the coverage view, AI drafts with review,
  and Explorer always complete.
- **Inconsistent facts between versions:** the stale flag, and the AI draft's check for names, dates and numbers.
- **Presets that feel like labels:** name what each one gives, not who the visitor is; make it easy to switch; Skip = Explorer.
- **Lenses with too little content:** hide the chip row under 2 tagged blocks.
