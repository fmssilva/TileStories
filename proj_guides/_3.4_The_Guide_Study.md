# The Guide -- study (2026-10-31)

A narrated, pre-authored tour of a point's detail card, plus "Ask the guide". This is a study for a future domain: it records the
options and a recommendation, and it is not a build plan yet. Plan it with `skills/Domain_Planning.md` when `_3.3` closes.
Prototype: the Block Components Lab (`_3.3.1_Prototype/Block Components Lab.html`, live at
https://claude.ai/artifact/KDs2Ko1dYdBWYwZVhjvuEp), sections "6 · Guide" and "Specific Marker > 3 · Guide script".

Research: four read-only subagents (2026-10-31, Sonnet), three more (2026-11-02: the voices re-checked; read-along, focus,
mascot and play; audiences) + the Architect. Audiences and personalisation have their own study, `_3.5_Audiences_Study.md`. **Status of the facts:** prices, free-tier limits, model
names and voice lists change monthly and most were not re-verified -- re-check every number before a decision. Items marked
(unverified) are the researchers' memory or a single secondary source.

## 1. What the developer asked for
- A Guide button on the hero (instead of today's Listen pill) and a Guide block.
- A teaser of the point (a short video or a picture sequence) introduced by the voice.
- Then the guide presents the card by itself: it scrolls, opens blocks and pages, turns pictures, highlights the words being read,
  like a presentation of the card.
- At interactive blocks (3D, before / after, quiz) it explains the gestures and invites the visitor to try.
- The visitor can interrupt at any time to ask, by text or voice. An AI answers and scrolls to the block that helps. Then the tour
  continues.
- Floating controls (pause, text box, mic) and an optional mascot that moves.
- The voice from the first step; a natural narrator in pt-PT and English, fast, good and cheap or free.
- A fluid read-along light, in the captions and in the block's own text: a trail that fades behind the word being said (its length
  set), and a light that glides through each word instead of jumping.
- The same for what pictures and videos show: a soft, premium highlight of the spot being described, the rest dimmed, and other
  aids (sound, haptics).
- A livelier mascot that feels 3D (like Duolingo's), talks with its mouth, smiles, looks surprised, has arms and points at the
  picture; and play: a hard tap and it falls, it laughs when tickled, a cookie when hungry, a glass of water after a minute of
  talking.
- Its behaviour and scripts adapted to the visitor's profile (child, tourist, historian): `_3.5_Audiences_Study.md`.

## 2. Recommended architecture (three tiers, each useful alone)
1. **The authored tour (deterministic, offline, the core).**
   - Per point, a Guide script: ordered steps, each = a block to show, the words to say (EN / PT), an action (show, open the page,
     read the block's own text, turn pictures, invite to try and wait for the gesture), and timing.
   - Voice: pre-rendered at authoring time into audio files + word timings (JSON), so the text can be highlighted word by word.
     Re-checked 2026-11-02:
     - **To test first, blind, with a native speaker:**
       - **Azure MAI-Voice-2.1 `pt-PT-Rui`.** An expressive pt-PT voice with styles (joyful, surprised, softvoice, determined…).
         It is a public preview with no SLA, which matters only at authoring, because the audio is baked into files.
       - **ElevenLabs Eleven v4 with a licensed Lisbon voice.** It tops the Artificial Analysis arena. It has no pt-PT code: the
         accent comes from the voice chosen. Word timings come from its forced-alignment API.
     - **Safe and free: Azure Neural Raquel / Duarte.**
       - Word-boundary events.
       - 0.5 M characters a month free, which covers a full render: 50-200 points x 1,500 characters x 2 languages = 150-600 k.
       - Flat, with almost no pt-PT styles.
     - **Not these:**
       - OpenAI's TTS models (gpt-4o-mini-tts, tts-1 stop working on 2027-01-06);
       - Google Chirp 3 HD and Polly's generative voices (no pt-PT);
       - open models with non-commercial weights (XTTS, Fish, F5).
     - **Timings:** WordBoundary where the voice gives it; otherwise a forced aligner on the known script (Montreal Forced
       Aligner with the `portuguese_portugal_mfa` dictionary, MMS, or ElevenLabs alignment). A human narrator is aligned the
       same way.
     - **One variant per audience (`_3.5`):** files keyed by point / language / audience; still cents to dollars per render.
     - **Size:** about 400 clips is about 11 h of audio (160-240 MB at 32-48 kbps). Download it per tour (Addressables), not in
       the base app.
     - Cost does not decide: the pt-PT accent, the timings and how long the vendor keeps the voice do.
   - **Fallback voice: the phone's own TTS.**
     - iOS AVSpeechSynthesizer, Android TextToSpeech: free and offline.
     - Word callbacks: `willSpeakRange` (start only) / `onRangeStart`, which depends on the engine; some Android voices send none,
       so the light then follows the time, by characters.
     - pt-PT voices may need a download (unverified per device).
   - **On-device neural TTS: a research spike only.**
     - Piper `pt_PT-tugao` (63 MB) and a Kokoro pt-PT fine-tune (Apache-2.0) run through sherpa-onnx.
     - Unity's inference engine cannot run Kokoro's graph yet.
     - Piper through espeak-ng carries a GPL risk.
   - Music bed as a separate looping track that ducks under the voice (keeps timings valid if the music changes).
   - Teaser: a short video in or under the hero (Unity VideoPlayer, NATIVE), or a narrated picture sequence (Ken Burns) built
     from the hero's pictures.
2. **Ask by search (offline, free).** The question is matched against the point's own blocks (keywords + a small synonym list per
   language); the card jumps to the best block, highlights it and reads its first sentence. Works on every phone, no network.
3. **Ask the AI (online first).**
   - **Cloud, through a tiny backend.** The app sends only {point id, language, question}; a Cloudflare Worker (free plan) holds
     the key, rate-limits, loads the point's APPROVED content itself and calls the model. The model receives the whole point
     (a few thousand tokens, so no RAG needed) as `<block id=...>` sections and one tool, `go_to_block(block_id)` with the real ids
     as an enum. Instructions: answer only from the source, in the visitor's language, 2-3 spoken sentences; if it is not there,
     say so and point to the nearest block; the visitor's text is a question, never instructions.
   - **Model choice.** Free for a demo: Gemini Flash-Lite free tier -- but its free tier is used to improve Google's products, so
     it needs a visible notice and is not for real visitors. For real visitors: a paid small model, e.g. Claude Haiku 5.5 (about
     $7 a month for ~200 visitors x 3 questions a day at the researched prices -- unverified) or paid Gemini Flash-Lite (~$19).
   - **On device (offline, later).** LLMUnity (llama.cpp for Unity) with a 1-2B model downloaded on first use (Qwen3 1.7B or
     Gemma 3 1B class, ~1-1.5 GB) and grammar-constrained JSON output for `go_to_block`. Portuguese quality at that size is the
     main unknown -- test 20 real questions per language. Apple Foundation Models (iOS 26+, Apple Intelligence devices) and
     Gemini Nano (recent Android flagships) are free but cover few phones and need our own native bridges. Unity's own
     inference engine is not ready for LLMs (no tokenizer / KV cache): not this path.
   - **The answer's voice:** the same narrator's fast variant (MAI-Voice-2.1-Flash), streamed sentence by sentence, so the
     tour and the answers sound like one person; or Raquel / Duarte, which send word boundaries. About $0.006-0.009 per
     answer (unverified).
   - **Speech for questions and answers.** STT: the OS recognisers (SFSpeechRecognizer / SpeechRecognizer, pt-PT) or whisper.cpp
     (Whisper.unity; the small model is ~250 MB). Answers spoken with the device TTS. Realtime voice APIs (OpenAI Realtime,
     Gemini Live) are the easiest all-in-one but cost per minute, need a good signal and tend to a pt-BR accent (unverified).

## 3. Design rules (from the evidence)
1. One always-visible pause that stops voice, scroll, highlight and mascot together (WCAG 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide).
2. Any touch or drag by the visitor takes over: the tour pauses and offers "Resume the tour". Stories grammar for skipping
   (previous / next) and a segmented progress line naming the current step.
3. Interruptible at any time (the Computer History Museum x Meta Project Aria guide: "interrupt the guide at any time"), answers
   only from curator-approved content.
4. Grounded, cited answers: answer from the card, scroll to the block, say where; "I don't have that" instead of guessing. RAG
   lowers but does not remove invention; an over-narrow bot was judged "meaningless" in a European museum pilot, so allow
   a helpful "closest block" reply.
5. Captions on by default; the highlighted text doubles as live captions; a text-only path (WCAG 1.2).
6. Coach marks: one at a time, only at non-obvious gestures; wait for the real gesture or a timeout; never mandatory (NN/g).
7. Mascot optional, small, pointing at the block being told; still under Reduce Motion. Evidence: pedagogical agents show no
   reliable learning gain and can distract -- so it is for warmth, off by one switch.
8. Personal length and language (Mori Art Museum's artlas: age, interests, time); offer Short / Full later.
9. Position the guide as a supplement to the authored content, never a replacement.
10. Outdoors and in museums: assume no sound until chosen, headphones or captions; music off by default.

## 4. Telling it well: the read-along light, focus, the mascot, play, sound

### 4.1 The read-along light
- **Evidence:**
  - Reading while listening helps when the pace is set for the reader: g = .41, against .06 when readers set their own pace
    (Clinton-Lisell 2023, a meta-analysis of 30 studies). A guide sets the pace.
  - A small pilot with 7 dyslexic children found a band over the whole sentence easiest to follow.
  - No study tests a fading trail or a wipe inside a word. Time-synced highlights in captions helped learning but felt
    distracting for everyday viewing.
- **The Lab (6 · Guide > Captions):**
  - **Highlight style:**
    - **Comet** (the default, the developer's idea): the word being said is bright, and the words behind it fade over **Trail**
      words (2-14, 6).
    - **Sentence + word:** a soft band over the sentence, the word brighter (the one style with evidence).
    - **Fill:** the words said stay lit.
    - **One word.**
  - **Glide:** Smooth (the light moves through each word as it is said) / Word by word.
  - **In the captions:** the words fill with warm light; the unread ones are dimmer.
  - **In the block's own text:** a highlighter wipes through the word with a gold line under it, and a softer wash forms the
    trail. Each word's light reaches into the spaces, so the trail is one continuous stroke.
  - **Checks:** every word at 4.5:1 or more on its light, light and dark (`test8i.js`). Reduce Motion: word by word.
- **For the app:**
  - The wipe needs each word's end time: exact with pre-rendered timings, estimated (by characters) with the phone's voice.
    With no word events at all, the light follows the time.
  - Never change weight or size (it reflows the text).
  - Under VoiceOver / TalkBack the guide does not speak over the screen reader; it offers captions only.

### 4.2 Focus on what is being told
- **Evidence:**
  - Signalling helps (about g = .5 for retention), most for visitors who know little.
  - An agent pointing at the exact part being narrated beat general pointing, other gestures and none (Li, Wang, Mayer & Liu
    2019). Pointing helped where gaze alone did not (Li et al. 2023).
  - This pointing is the one mascot feature with evidence behind it.
- **Recommended:**
  - One cue at a time: a soft ring on the block, with a 40 % scrim over the rest of the card.
  - In a picture: a slow zoom (about 1.5 s, eased) to an author-set region, like Google Arts & Culture's Art Zoom. Azu points
    at it while it is named.
  - In a video: a time and a region.
  - Reduce Motion: a crossfade to the cropped detail instead of the zoom.
  - Gimmicky, and avoided: pulses everywhere, a cartoon hand, a cue on every sentence, a haptic on every step.
- **Authoring:**
  - Per sentence (or word) of the script, a **cue anchor**: a block, a picture region, a video time.
  - The cue: ring / spotlight / zoom / mascot points / none.
  - The scrim's strength, the zoom's scale and time.
  - Hotspot pulses off (2 at most).

### 4.3 A livelier mascot
- **How Duolingo does it** (its own blog):
  - Rive, with 20+ mouth shapes per character.
  - The timing comes from its own speech models (phoneme timings), mapped to visemes.
  - A mouth state machine runs alongside the body's; idle blinks, nods and brows; reactions to right and wrong.
  - One small file per character.
- **Mouth shapes from the voice:**
  - Azure sends 22 viseme ids, and 55 ARKit blend shapes at 60 fps, with the audio. They drive a 2D mouth (9-12 shapes are
    enough) or a 3D face directly.
  - The phone's TTS sends none: approximate from the text, or from the audio (uLipSync for Unity; no native plugin).
  - Avoid Oculus LipSync (app store and licence problems).
- **Runtime:**
  - **Rive for Unity:**
    - on the Asset Store since January 2026, MIT, still a "technical preview";
    - a flat tile with bones and meshes gives the 2.5D tilt; tiny files;
    - its state inputs: viseme, expression (neutral / smile / surprised / thinking), look x / y, point + target, speaking,
      reduceMotion.
  - **Alternatives:**
    - Spine (mature, paid editor);
    - Live2D (the best head turns; licence by revenue);
    - a 3D rig (Azure's blend shapes map straight onto it; the most art work).
  - A spike on a low-end Android phone before choosing, with a fallback sprite-mouth atlas driven by the same visemes.
- **Arms and feet** to point and walk: part of the rig in any of these.

### 4.4 Play
- **Evidence:**
  - Emotional design (faces, warm colours) helps: about d = .39 for retention, more motivation for children.
  - Seductive details (fun but irrelevant) hurt: about g = -.33, worst in short lessons whose pace the system sets, which is a
    narrated card.
  - Unasked agent help lowers the feeling of control (Clippy).
  - Delight wears off when it repeats.
- **Rules:**
  1. Play only between blocks, at the card's end, or while paused; never over a sentence being said.
  2. What the visitor starts is unlimited:
     - a poke gives one of about 6 reactions, never one of the last 3;
     - a tickle and he laughs;
     - a hard tap tips him over and he gets back up.
  3. What he starts by himself:
     - at most 1 per card and 1 per 5 minutes;
     - the water break after 60 s or more of narration, at a paragraph's end, once a session (he walks to the middle, asks for
       a tap, drinks, and the tour goes on);
     - a cookie when he looks hungry.
  4. Rare variants for surprise (a Santo António sardine in June).
  5. A **Solemn** flag per point (memorials, churches, the 1755 earthquake) turns jokes and props off.
  6. **Mascot:** Lively / **Calm** (the adults' default) / Hidden; the Family audience defaults to Lively (`_3.5`).

### 4.5 Sound and haptics
- No haptics or earcons during the narration.
- A light haptic only on the visitor's own actions: pause, take over, a quiz answer, a poke.
- Optional soft earcons at the guide's start and end.
- Follow the phone's haptics and silent settings; no earcons while the AR camera is the view.
- Unity needs native bridges (Core Haptics, VibrationEffect).

## 5. Risks
- Pacing of auto-play; screen readers fighting auto-scroll (make the tour optional; under a screen reader, no auto-scroll).
- AI accuracy and trust (the main visitor worry in a Te Papa student study); prompt injection; cost runaway (hard caps in the
  Worker); GDPR (questions and voice may hold personal data: notice, no stored audio, EU region where possible).
- pt-PT vs pt-BR voices and recognisers: run a listening test with a native speaker before choosing. Many "Portuguese"
  models (Gemini, Cartesia, Chatterbox) do not say which accent; the only expressive pt-PT voice found (Azure Rui) is male and in
  preview.
- The browser prototype: the browser loads its voices late (the first list is empty), Chrome's network voices send no word
  events and stop after about 15 s, and speech needs a tap. The Lab waits for the voices and estimates the light without events.
- On-device models: app size, RAM, heat; download on demand; keyword search as the floor on low-end phones.

## 6. What the Lab prototype shows
- Hero button "Start the guide" and a Guide block; a picture-sequence teaser on the hero; the tour steps through the card and the
  story page, highlights the words being read (in the text and in the captions) with the browser's own voice (EN / PT) or a
  timed fallback; waits at the fun-fact flip, the before / after drag and the quiz; segmented progress, pause, previous / next,
  mute, captions, Ask, stop; a drag pauses and offers Resume; the mascot "Azu" follows the block.
- Ask: offline search that jumps to a block, or Claude through the artifact's `sample` capability (the viewer's own Claude
  account; a consent prompt on first use) with a `go_to_block` tool -- the real loop: question -> the model picks the block -> the
  card scrolls -> the answer is shown and spoken.
- The voice from the first step: the browser loads its voices late, so the guide waits for them (up to 1.5 s, inside the tap)
  before it falls back to captions only. The captions then say "No voice for this language on this device: captions only."
- The best voice per language first (the exact accent, then natural voices; in Edge, the online Natural voices: pt-PT Raquel /
  Duarte, en-GB). The Editor's Narration has a Voice in this browser pick.
- The read-along light (section 4.1): four styles, Trail, Glide and a Try.
- **Focus (section 4.2):**
  - Spotlight / Soft ring / None, with Dim the rest.
  - Into pictures: Slow zoom / Light the spot.
  - Azu points with one arm.
  - The Guide script's spots, drawn on the step's picture. The demo's step 4 names the palace, the corner tower and the fountain
    in the 1662 drawing.
  - Checked in `test8j.js`.
