Welcome to Saint George's Castle, the fortress that watches over Lisbon.
You are standing on the highest hill of the old city, and from here you can see the whole river.
People lived on this hill long before Portugal existed.
In the eleventh century the Moors fortified it,
and its stone walls and towers still follow the line of that old citadel.
In the year eleven forty seven, Afonso Henriques, the first king of Portugal, arrived with an army.
Crusaders on their way to the Holy Land joined him,
and together they besieged the town for about four months.
When the town fell, the castle changed hands for good.
Soon it became a royal residence, the Alcáçova palace.
For centuries the kings of Portugal lived inside these walls, among halls, gardens and courtyards.
Around the year fifteen hundred, King Manuel the First moved the court down to the river,
and the castle slowly lost its royal role.
It served as a barracks, and even as a prison.
Then, in seventeen fifty five, the great earthquake shook down much of what was left.
The walls you walk on today were largely rebuilt in the nineteen thirties and forties.
Look for the eleven towers, and for the keep, the strongest tower,
where the defenders made their last stand.
Walk along the wall, and notice how thick the stone is.
Peacocks wander the gardens, and in the Ulysses Tower a camera obscura shows the city moving on a white dish.
A legend says that a knight called Martim Moniz died holding a gate open for the Christian army.
Whether or not it is true, the little gate still carries his name.
Before you leave, look down at the Baixa, the grid of straight streets rebuilt after the earthquake,
and at the Alfama, the tangle of narrow lanes that the earthquake spared.
Both are Lisbon, and this hill has watched over them for nine centuries.
Thank you for visiting.
