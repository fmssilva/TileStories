Welcome to the Terreiro do Paço, the Palace Yard.
Stand here, at the edge of the Tagus, and imagine a palace in front of you.
Not a castle on a hill, but a royal home built right on the water.
Around the year fifteen hundred, King Manuel the First left the old castle above the city and moved down to the river.
The reason was simple: this is where the money arrived.
Ships from India and Brazil tied up beside the palace,
and the king could watch the spices, the sugar and the gold come ashore from his own windows.
Around the palace grew offices, workshops, a chapel and the royal archive.
It became the true centre of the kingdom.
Later kings added to it.
In the fifteen eighties, when Philip the First ruled Portugal and Spain together,
the river front received a new facade and a domed corner tower, the Torreão.
Sailors saw that tower long before they saw the city.
Inside, the palace kept one of the richest libraries in Europe, said to hold tens of thousands of books,
along with paintings, maps and a royal opera house.
That opera house, the Ópera do Tejo, opened in March of seventeen fifty five.
It stood for just eight months.
On the first of November, seventeen fifty five, All Saints' Day, the ground began to shake.
The earthquake brought the palace walls down.
Then a great wave came up the river, and fire spread through what was left.
The royal family was away from the building that morning,
but the library, the archive and almost everything inside were lost in a single day.
Lisbon was rebuilt on a new, strict plan.
Where the palace had stood, an open square was laid out, facing the river like a grand front door.
We call it the Praça do Comércio, the Commerce Square.
Look at the yellow arcades around you, and at the statue of King Joseph the First in the middle.
Yet many Lisboetas still use the old name, Terreiro do Paço, as if the palace were still standing.
On the tile panel, and in the old paintings, you can see what stood here.
Take a moment to compare the picture from before the earthquake with the square in front of you.
