# World Design (4.1.4) -- Wall Life: animated content on the wall (planning, 2026-10-29)

**What this file is.** Planning for the third kind of world content, after the point marker (exists) and the Region
(`_4.1.3`): **living content placed on the wall**. Examples are windows and doors that open, curtains in the wind, a woman
hanging laundry, a horse walking down the street, a tree swaying, birds in the sky, a mascot that drops in now and then. It
builds on `_4.1.1` sections 4.4 (World Content prefab and World Moment), 4.5 (wall-level content), 4.7 (what stays
external) and 4.8 (option C). It contains no code. The next step is a prototype in the same format as the Region Editor Lab.

**The developer's question:** are these all the same type? Short answer:
- **No for the artist:** they are made in different ways.
- **Yes for the framework:** each one is a piece of Unity content anchored to the wall, with a schedule of when it plays.

The framework does not need to know how a piece animates inside (Animator, Timeline, particles, a video, a 2D rig). It
needs to know **where the piece is attached** and **when it plays**. The kinds below exist so that the Editor can offer the
right anchor, mask and material helpers, and the right starting template.

---

## 1. Six kinds, on two axes

| Axis | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Where it lives** | On the wall surface (flat), out of the wall (3D pieces hinged on it), in the space in front of the wall (sky, street), or on the screen |
| **How it is made** | A shader on the wall image, 2D art (layers, a rig, a flipbook, a video), a 3D model with animation, particles |

| # | Kind | Lives | Made with (normal tools) | Examples | Precision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | **Repaint** (restyle or relight the wall) | on the surface, covering a region | the painted wall image: Krita/Photoshop over the exported **wall canvas** (section 3), or AI style transfer; shader reveal/blend in Unity | cartoon or expressionist facade, night lighting, windows glowing, then/now colour | high (it covers the real facade); hidden by feather and by a strong style |
| K2 | **Flat animation** (2D on the wall) | on the surface, flush, inside the region | PSD layers -> Unity **PSD Importer + 2D Animation** (bones) -> Animation window; or a flipbook; or a video with alpha (After Effects, Rive) | curtain in the wind, laundry, a tree swaying, a horse in profile walking along the street, flags | medium (needs its window or street, not every pixel) |
| K3 | **Hinged 3D piece** (the wall itself moves) | out of the wall, starting flush | **ProBuilder** in Unity (or Blender): a thin slab cut to the window or door shape, pivot on the hinge, wearing the **wall-projection material** (it shows the real wall photo, so closed it is invisible); animated in the Animation window | shutters and doors that open, a balcony that unfolds, a tile that flips | high (closed it must match the real one); stylised art helps |
| K4 | **Agent on a path** | in front of the wall, moving | any prefab (2D flipbook or 3D model) + Unity Splines' **Spline Animate** on a Path shape drawn in the Region tool | a horse or tram along a street, a cat on a ledge, a boat on the river | low (a path a few cm off still reads) |
| K5 | **Ambient particles** | in front of the wall or in the sky | **Particle System** whose emitter is a Region shape (polygon or path) or a box | birds flocking over the roofs, leaves, smoke from a chimney, dust in a light beam, rain | low |
| K6 | **Screen guest** (mascot) | on the screen, not in the world | UI Toolkit (or uGUI) with a flipbook, a 2D rig or Rive | a mascot peeking in from the edge, a seagull crossing, a guide that points | none |

What changes between the kinds: the **anchor** (surface rectangle, hinge, path, emitter shape, screen edge), the **masking**
(clip to a region, cover the real element, hide behind the wall), the **starting template** the Editor creates, and the
**precision** needed.

What does not change: each one is **one row in a World Content table**, has **a prefab** (option C: real animation lives
in prefabs, referenced by path) and **a playback schedule**.

## 2. Playback: when content plays

This is one shared model for all kinds. It is the World Moment of `_4.1.1` section 4.4, made concrete.

| Trigger | Meaning | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| `reveal` | once, when the POI appears (after its level's reveal delay) | a door opens to welcome you |
| `loop` | always while visible | a tree swaying, curtains |
| `focus` | when the visitor taps the POI | shutters open, interior lights up |
| `unfocus` | when the card closes | shutters close again |
| `every` | at random, every N to M seconds | birds fly over, the mascot drops in, a horse passes |
| `lapse` | when the time period changes (the `_0_work_plan` lapse/epoch timeline) | the 1755 facade swaps for today's |

Per row: trigger, delay, how many times (once, loop), and the state under **reduce motion** (static frame, or hidden).

Inside the prefab, the row's trigger simply calls one of the following: an **Animator trigger** of the same name, a
**Timeline** (`PlayableDirector`), `ParticleSystem.Play`, or `VideoPlayer.Play`. That is Unity's own vocabulary, with no
private step list.

## 3. The key framework helper: the wall canvas

**Problem:** an artist painting a curtain for window 3 must paint it at exactly the right place and scale. Doing that by eye
in AR is impossible for a beginner.

**Proposal:** one button in the POI Editor, **Export wall canvas**.
1. It renders the GLB region of the POI (or the whole wall) orthographically, straight on, to a PNG at a known scale. The
   Region outlines are drawn on a separate layer.
2. The artist paints in Krita or Photoshop *over that image*, one layer per moving part (curtain, shutter, laundry).
3. They save a PSD or PSB next to the POI (`POIs/<poi_id>/world/`).
4. **Import wall art** puts it back on a quad with exactly the same rectangle. The layers land in place, the 2D Animation
   rig works on them as usual, and nothing needs to be aligned by hand.

The same canvas feeds K1 (the repainted facade is one layer), K2 (moving layers), K3 (the shutter shape and the
wall-projection texture) and video (After Effects or Rive composed over the canvas, exported with alpha at the same size).

This one helper makes "art that sits exactly on a building" doable without 3D skills. It also matches the rule in
`_4.1.1` section 4.7: we add it because it needs the wall.

## 4. Masks and materials the framework provides

| Helper | Why | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| **Region clip** | the content shows only inside a Region shape (a curtain stays in its window) | K1, K2, video |
| **Cover patch** | paints over the real element so it can move: a closed real window must disappear when the animated one opens. It is filled from the wall canvas (the painted "closed" or "interior" state) | K2, K3 |
| **Interior box** | a dark, shallow box with a warm light behind an opened window or door, so the hole has depth | K3 |
| **Wall occluder** | an invisible copy of the GLB that writes depth only, so a 3D piece or an agent passes *behind* the wall's edges and corners | K3, K4, K5 |
| **Wall-projection material** | a 3D piece shows the real wall photo from the canvas: closed, it is invisible; when it moves, the wall itself seems to move | K3 |
| **Edge feather** | softens the edge of a repaint, to hide alignment error and the mismatch with the real light | K1 |

## 5. Blind spots (newcomer warnings)

1. **To open a window you must first remove the real one.** The camera keeps showing the real closed window, so a Cover
   patch has to hide it. A photoreal patch will mismatch the real light at another time of day. **A clearly artistic style
   (cartoon, painted) hides that mismatch.** This is the strongest reason to choose stylised art over photoreal.
2. **People and cars do not hide AR content.** Someone walking in front of the wall will not cover the curtain. Keep
   content flush on the wall, or accept it. ARCore's Depth API could help on some phones; not now.
3. **Parallax:** flat content (K1, K2) is exact at any viewing angle because it lies on the wall. Content in front of the
   wall (K4, K5) moves against the wall as you walk. That is correct, and it is what sells the 3D.
4. **Weight and performance:**
   - one video decoder at a time (`_4.1.1`);
   - skinned 2D rigs are cheap;
   - many large transparent quads are not (overdraw, heat);
   - every prefab adds to the build until Addressables (Stage 6).
5. **Attention:** a wall full of motion is noise, and seductive details hurt learning (`_4.1.1` section 8). The budget
   rule: **at most one looping animation in view per POI, plus rare ambient events.** The mascot appears rarely and never
   during a card.
6. **Rights:** repainting a historic facade is fine. Repainting a living artist's mural is not.

## 6. Where it goes in the Editor (to prove with the prototype)

```
GLOBAL SCENE
  Wall Life                                     [NEW]
    Wall-level content table: name | kind | prefab | anchor | trigger | (x)
      (birds over the roofs, the mascot, rain)
    Budget: max loops in view, mascot min interval, reduce-motion behaviour
    > Test: Scene / Playmode / Device guides

SPECIFIC MARKER > POI > World Content           [NEW, next to Region]
  [Export wall canvas]  [Import wall art]
  Content table: name | kind | prefab | anchor (shape) | trigger | delay | (edit prefab) (x)
    curtain_left   Flat animation   Curtain.prefab    Windows / #3   loop
    shutters       Hinged 3D        Shutters.prefab   Window 3       focus / unfocus
    horse          Agent on path    Horse.prefab      Street         every 20-40 s
  > Selected row: kind-specific helpers (region clip, cover patch, interior box, occluder),
    reduce-motion state, the folder (POIs/<poi_id>/world/)
```

A new row creates the prefab from a **kind template**, in the POI's folder (`_4.1.1` section 4.9), and opens it in Prefab
Mode in Context, with the wall around it.

## 7. Prototype: Wall Life Lab (next)

It uses the same format as the Region Editor Lab: the painting as the wall, a Unity-skinned Editor, and ▶ Play. One working
example per kind, each with a few parameters:

| Kind | Example on the painting |
|---|---|
| K1 Repaint | the Golden Palace region restyled (posterised "cartoon" or night relight), revealed with a wipe on `reveal` |
| K2 Flat animation | a curtain in a "window" of the painting, waving in the wind (`loop`), with a Cover patch behind it |
| K3 Hinged 3D | two shutters on the Cobalt Tower that swing open in perspective on `focus`, wearing the wall image, with a warm interior behind |
| K4 Agent on path | a small horse or tram silhouette walking along the Navy Gate street path, `every` 15-30 s |
| K5 Particles | a flock of birds crossing the top of the frame, and leaves falling from a region |
| K6 Screen guest | a mascot peeking in from the screen edge, rarely, never while the card is open |
